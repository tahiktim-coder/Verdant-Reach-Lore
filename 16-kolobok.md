# Kolobok: the Invisible Loaf

Kolobok is the third sister project: a huge invisible loaf that rolls the roads and fields of the Reach, the stone and pebble folk blamed for it, and the flour that shows it and feeds it. Your premise is [CANON]; the backstory, names and signs are the studio Claude's proposals. Related: [15-still-water.md](15-still-water.md), [07-creatures-and-peoples.md](07-creatures-and-peoples.md), [03-knights-and-orders.md](03-knights-and-orders.md), [06-places.md](06-places.md).

## Premise

- **[CANON]** (studio lore) A huge, invisible being inspired by Kolobok, the runaway round bun of the folk tale. It terrorises roads and fields.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) It only becomes visible if you blow a lot of flour on it.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) Pebble people and stone people hate it: humans kept attacking them, thinking its damage was their doing.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) They learned a few words of human tongue to say it isn't theirs: **“he is not ours”**. In the games this became the two words **NOT OURS**. See S14 in [10-contradictions-and-fixes.md](10-contradictions-and-fixes.md).
- **[GAME TEXT]** The prototype's title screen calls it “A tale from the Verdant Reach”, which places the loaf inside the Reach. Muster lore line: “No one can take it. No one can see it. Flour shows it, and flour feeds it.” Home: “The roads”.

## Origin: the fish's wish [STUDIO PROPOSAL]

Proposed by the studio's Claude, built into the prototype, not confirmed by you.

- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** A famine winter. An old woman scraped the bins and floor cracks for the last flour and baked one round loaf.
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Years before, she had caught the talking fish at Still Water and saved her wish. She wished: “Let no one ever take this bread from us.” Granted word for word: no one can take it, catch it, eat it, or see it.
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** It rolled off the windowsill while cooling and never stopped, including away from her.
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Why it works in the Reach: magic doesn't work there, but a wish isn't a spell, so there is nothing to dispel. See S2.
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Whatever it rolls over sticks to the dough. It is hill-sized and still growing. It isn't hunting: like the tale, it is always running away, and at that size running away flattens the road. In the prototype its radius is 15 metres before anyone feeds it.

## Names

- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Road folk call it **the Loaf**. Stone folk call it **Soft** (“SOFT ONE. NOT OURS.”). The project name **Kolobok** is yours; it is also the roster's creature name.

## Signs of the loaf

- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Round flattened swaths through wheat overnight. Warm bread smell on an empty road (go uphill, it is slow uphill). Crows standing on nothing, walking in place on its crust. Foxes trailing it (“Every loaf meets its fox”).
- **[GAME TEXT]** (from Reach Field Tips) Card 1: “If the road smells of warm bread and nothing’s on it, get uphill. It’s slow uphill.” Card 11: “If the crows on a fence all go quiet at once, watch the road, not the sky.” Card 5: “If a flour bell bursts and nobody’s near it, don’t stay to see what’s there.”
- **[GAME TEXT]** (from the prototype) Opening captions: “Warm bread on the air. Nothing on the road.” “The crows are standing on nothing.” Its track is a round pressed trail through waist-high wheat, stalks laid down along the roll. A low sound in the mix is “the loaf’s weight on the ground”.
- **[GAME TEXT]** **Loaf Crows** (Muster): “They ride what no one can see, pecking crumbs off its crust.” Tap lines: “Kraa.” and “They’re walking on nothing again.” They walk against the roll to stay on top.

## Flour and flour bells

- **[CANON]** (studio lore) A lot of flour makes it visible.
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** It shows as a white hill rolling for a few breaths. Then the dough drinks the flour: every sack you throw to see it makes it bigger. **[GAME TEXT]** Captions: “It drinks the flour.” “It’s bigger now.” “Every sack makes it bigger.” Muster growth stat: “+1 size a sack”.
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** **Road wardens** hang **flour bells**, sacks of flour on poles, over roads. When the loaf hits one it bursts and coats it. **[GAME TEXT]** “The flour bell burst.” “There it is.” A burst bell is a snapped pole and an empty, torn sack.
- **[GAME TEXT]** (from Hero of the Reach) A Verdant Knight carries a **Flour Sack**: “For the roads at harvest. Throw it hard, and don’t stay to watch.” So in the game the Order knows the trick.
- **[GAME TEXT]** Only the floured parts cast a shadow. Flour on it reads thin and damp where it is being drunk. **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Where it has rolled over pebble folk, flour shows dark lumps in the crust, some with their eyes still open (the prototype draws pebble folk stuck in the crust).

## The song

- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Its song is a muffled boast listing everyone it rolled away from, heard as if through bread. Hear your village in the verse and it has already been there.
- **[GAME TEXT]** Verses: “I rolled from the bin and I rolled from the sill, / I rolled from the miller, I rolled from the mill, / I rolled from the knights and the stones on the hill, / and I’ll roll from you. I am rolling still.” After it is fed: “I rolled from your flour, and I’m hungry still.” The Fed ending: “It left singing a new verse. The verse is about you.”

## The pebble folk and the stone folk, and NOT OURS

- **[CANON]** (studio lore) Both peoples hate the loaf because humans blamed them for its damage, and both learned a few human words to deny it.
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Pebble folk live on the Still Water shore. Stone folk are field and boundary stones. Elders are tall standing stones with two eyes that mill grain between two of their kind. Their one phrase is NOT OURS: pebbles clack it, elders cut it into their own faces. Masons were paid per stone; the Not ours ending implies they were paid to break blamed stones. The stone folk, natural millers, taught humans the flour trick knowing it feeds the loaf.
- **[GAME TEXT]** Pebble Folk (Muster and Kolobok): “Roll to travel. Blamed for the Loaf’s tracks, so they learned two human words.” Lines: “not ours not ours not ours”, “not ours!”, “not ours, not ours”. Stone Elder: “Mills grain between two of its kind. Cut the only phrase it knows into its own face.” Lines: “NOT OURS.” “NOT. OURS.” “SOFT ONE. NOT OURS.” “GO. NOT OURS.” Stone-folk eyes are mint green in the prototype's palette.
- **[GAME TEXT]** (from Reach Field Tips) Card 2: “If a standing stone stares at you, apologise. It knows two words, and it’s tired of saying them.” Card 6: “If the pebbles on a lakeshore blink at you, don’t skip them across the water. They remember.” Card 12: “Stone folk will mill your grain for free. Never ask what they do with the flour.”
- **[PROPOSED FIX]** Relation to the bible's Stonefolk (tiny, tipped with pebbles, hurt by rain): see S6.

## The hook: a talking job

- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** The knights won their covenant with wordplay; the fox in the tale won with flattery. Stopping the loaf may be a talking job, not a fight. This ties the loaf to the covenant origin in [03-knights-and-orders.md](03-knights-and-orders.md) and to the fish's word-for-word wishes. Not yours; ask before using.

## Prototype mechanics and endings [GAME TEXT]

- **[GAME TEXT]** You stand on a hilltop 24 m above a wheat valley at dusk beside a leaning stone elder and four pebble folk; below, a road with cart ruts, strip fields, a windmill, a lake at the horizon. The loaf rolls a loop through the wheat with five crows on its crust.
- **[GAME TEXT]** Nine sacks of flour, the village's whole supply (“No flour left in the village.”). A flour bell bursts once and shows it. Hits coat it, it casts a long shadow, crows scatter, then it drinks the flour and grows 0.8 m per hit. Tap the stone elder to hear it; tap a pebble and they chatter. Prompts: “Tap it to sling flour”, “Aim where the crows stand”. Miss: “Flour on the wheat. Nothing under it.” “That was the last sack.” “It takes the road.”
- **[GAME TEXT]** Endings. **Not ours** (0 hits): “Nine sacks on the wheat and not one on the loaf. Tomorrow a mason gets paid for another stone.” **Seen** (1 to 4): “You saw it roll, and so did the road. Nobody blames the stones tonight. It’s a little bigger for it.” **Fed** (5 or more): “You saw it every time, and every time it drank the flour. It left singing a new verse. The verse is about you.”
- **[GAME TEXT]** Roster stats (Attack 25, Defense 40, Health 1200, “1?” available) are placeholders, never lore.
