# The Wider World

The Verdant Reach sits inside a much larger world of very different magical civilizations. The regions developed most often are the Reach itself, the mage-dominated South (the Mage Kingdoms), the Northern Lands, the pirate/artifact powers, the vampire networks, the ancient abandoned war regions and lost civilizations.

## The Northern Lands

### Magic

- **[CANON]** The North lies behind enormous mountains.
- **[CANON]** Outsiders may assume the North, like the Reach, has little magic. That's wrong. The North has **too much unstable magical interference**. Wild magical currents move constantly through the air. Ordinary spells don't just weaken there. They break before they can properly form.

### Crystal magic

- **[CANON]** Northerners use magical crystals of different strengths and levels.
- **[CANON]** Northern casting works like this:
  1. Draw energy from the crystal.
  2. Use part of that energy to build a stabilizing, protective layer around the forming magic.
  3. Form the actual spell inside that protection.
  4. Release it.
- **[CANON]** Crystals do more than store power like batteries. They let magic survive hostile magical weather.

### Sacred trees

- **[CANON]** Ancient sacred trees slowly produce or grow magical crystalline material, sometimes over extremely long periods. Crystals store accumulated magical and natural energy, and using them drains them.
- **[CANON]** The chain is: nature → sacred trees → crystals → controlled magic.
- **[PROVISIONAL]** Earlier version: enormous blue magical trees whose roots draw energy from deep in the earth and pass it into crystals. The visuals changed over time, but the core link remains.

### Magical storms

- **[CANON]** Some northern valleys hold moving or semi-permanent magical storms, dangerous enough that even Northerners avoid them. They aren't thunderstorms with magic effects. They are places where already unstable currents become catastrophic.
- Open: what lies inside the storm valleys.

### Northern politics (early version)

- **[PROVISIONAL]** The earliest version had four northern kingdoms, one of which could be cut off by the seasons.
- **[PROVISIONAL]** The North was harsh, less populous than the South, politically fragmented and semi-independent, more used to ancient machinery, and closely tied to blue trees and crystals.
- **[PROVISIONAL]** It's unclear whether the four-kingdom structure survived. Treat it as reusable early lore.

### The north path (studio lore)

- **[CANON]** "In the north, a mountain path leads to an unknown sea and a strip of land." (studio lore, [his])
- **[CANON]** Both statements are canon and both stand: the North lies behind enormous mountains (bible, above), and a mountain path leads north out of the Reach (studio). How the "unknown sea" sits beside the crystal-magic Northern Lands is contradiction S8 in [10-contradictions-and-fixes.md](10-contradictions-and-fixes.md).
- **[PROPOSED FIX]** The path crosses the mountains. The sea and the strip of land are "unknown" from the Reach's side because Reach maps stop at the mountains. The strip of land could be the coast the northern kingdoms live on, which would also explain the earliest version's one northern kingdom that the seasons cut off. Not canon until you approve it.
- Open: whether the strip of land is the Northern Lands themselves, a coast in front of them, or something else; whether Northerners know the sea; and whether the Reach's own far north, where the Starblooms grow, lies before or beyond the path. See [11-open-questions.md](11-open-questions.md).

## The South and the Mage Kingdoms

- **[CANON]** The South is more populous and politically dominant than the North. It is rich in ambient magic, historically ruled by mages, and home to powerful magical institutions. Possible features: magical aristocracies, politics controlled by mages, and strong magical armies.
- **[CANON]** Modern mage society exists **after overthrowing an older arcane-mechanical civilization**. The people who treat magic as the foundation of society live on top of a civilization whose technology may have surpassed theirs. See [05-ancient-history.md](05-ancient-history.md).
- **[PROVISIONAL]** In an early version, mage society was overwhelmingly dominant but starting to crack politically.
- **[CANON]** Another version describes the rest of the continent as **multiple Mage Kingdoms** with stable ambient magic and conventional high-output mages. They form most of the continent's ordinary political arena. The Reach stands outside that system.
- **[PROPOSED FIX]** "The South" and "the Mage Kingdoms" are the same space described at different zoom levels. The South is the region, and the Mage Kingdoms are the states within it. See [10-contradictions-and-fixes.md](10-contradictions-and-fixes.md).
- **[CANON]** Many of the Mage Kingdoms are associated with Y.

### The eastern kingdom (studio lore)

Your studio notes describe one big kingdom to the east of the Reach. How it relates to the South and the Mage Kingdoms above is contradiction S3 in [10-contradictions-and-fixes.md](10-contradictions-and-fixes.md); both versions are kept. The bullets quote your notes word for word because the wording is open to more than one reading.

- **[CANON]** "In the east lies one big kingdom full of mages." (studio lore, [his])
- **[CANON]** It is "built so its people sin, because sinning takes away their magic." (studio lore, [his])
- **[CANON]** "From outside it looks amazing: lots of food and fun." (studio lore, [his])
- **[CANON]** "The system exists to take people further from their God, so minor deities can rule over their kingdoms." (studio lore, [his]) Note the capital G and the plural "kingdoms".
- **[CANON]** "Legalised sins today: brothels, killings, a celebration day when no crime is punished, killing unwanted babies below a certain age, and a day of abundance where people indulge until they lose their minds." (studio lore, [his])
- **[GAME TEXT]** "If a kingdom throws you a feast the night you arrive, count the exits before the second course." (Reach Field Tips, card 9; the card's code calls it "the eastern kingdom's welcome feast")
- **[FLAVOR, from card 9]** The kingdom is shown as a city of towers and domes along the horizon at night, every window lit, a lamp-lined road leading to it, fireworks over it. The card's code calls the fireworks "a welcome too warm".
- **[PROPOSED FIX]** Read the eastern kingdom as the largest Mage Kingdom and the Reach's immediate eastern neighbour. The South's other Mage Kingdoms lie beyond it. The sin system belongs to this kingdom, not to every Mage Kingdom, though "their kingdoms" suggests the minor deities hold more than one. See S3.
- Open: who "their God" is; whether the minor deities are the same kind of being as the "gods" of the god-war era and the "dirty deceiver" who gave the cursed sword (see [05-ancient-history.md](05-ancient-history.md)); whether "their magic" means the people's magic or the mages'; whether Y and the sin system are connected. See [11-open-questions.md](11-open-questions.md).

## Y

*"Y" is a placeholder name.*

- **[CANON]** A population or group whose members, the Y-born:
  - lack certain ordinary human emotions,
  - share a subtle mental connection with one another,
  - can imitate ordinary behaviour,
  - often hide self-interested motives beneath that behaviour.
- **[CANON]** Y has infiltrated many societies. It holds important levers and influence and is widespread, but it does **not** secretly control the whole world and is not all-powerful.
- **[CANON]** **Y cannot properly dominate the Verdant Reach.** The reasons are its extremely old institutions, local cultures thousands of years old, strict customs, independent castles, secret societies, multiple knightly orders, limited involvement in world politics, strong local identities, and the ability of separate institutions to unite against existential interference. Its strength comes from systems outsiders cannot fully map.

## X

*"X" is a placeholder name.*

- **[CANON]** A society or group that resisted Y successfully for a time, helped by a mental or psychological advantage.
- **[CANON]** At its height, X grew strong enough to nearly dominate the wider world.
- **[CANON]** Pride and corruption eventually distorted that advantage, and X fell.
- **[CANON]** Survivors and remnants fled far away for about 100 years.
- Open: what the advantage was, why it corrupted, and what happened during the century in exile.

## The Pirate / Artifact Kingdom

- **[CANON]** A pirate civilization that isn't just sea raiders. Its rise is tied to artifacts. Pirates captured, recovered and traded powerful ancient objects, and artifact barter became a foundation of political legitimacy and economic power. Over time, piracy grew into something like a real kingdom.
- **[PROVISIONAL]** No official name yet.

## The Vampire Network

- **[CANON]** You wanted a vampire faction that avoids gothic vampire-kingdom clichés. It became a distributed vampire network embedded in trade, finance, politics, noble society, old families and international relationships, not a single obvious kingdom.
- **[PROVISIONAL]** Name: **Ashen Blood**. This name came out of our joint development and was never firmly locked.

## The Great Abandoned Warland

- **[CANON]** A region holding colossal ancient war machines, clockwork and steampunk-style machinery, destroyed castles, vast cemeteries and evidence of an unimaginable war. Nobody knows exactly what happened, and the region is largely avoided. Its scale should suggest that current civilization doesn't understand the forces that once fought there.
- **[PROVISIONAL]** Name: **the Silent Battlefield**. This was an assistant name and is less certain than the concept.

See also: the Endless Sword Fields and the clockwork towers in [06-places.md](06-places.md).

## The Librarians' three guilds (studio lore)

The Librarians are an Old Age institution that became three guilds, each with a capital in the present day: three more powers on the map.

- **[CANON]** The Librarians were reincarnating preservers of ancient secrets, magic and technology, commissioned by kings and rulers to unveil specific secrets of magic; each discovery took 4 to 5 reincarnations. (studio lore, [his])
- **[CANON]** Still in the Old Age, the Library suddenly and magically disappeared, and the reincarnated librarians founded an order. **[PROVISIONAL]** The order's name is undecided. (studio lore, [his] and [working])
- **[CANON]** Over thousands of years the order became three guilds, Mages, Engineers and Collectors, "each with its own capital today". (studio lore, [his])
- **[GAME TEXT]** The Muster's Collector and Reach Field Tips card 4 draw on this; quotes in [05-ancient-history.md](05-ancient-history.md).
