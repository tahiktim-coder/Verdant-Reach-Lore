# Creatures and Peoples

**Governing rule [CANON]:** The Verdant Reach is poor in ambient magic but extraordinarily rich in magical life. Many mythical creatures live there. See [02-magic.md](02-magic.md).

Many entries below came from TikTok/visual work and are tagged **[FLAVOR]**. They are usable world texture. Promote any of them to canon whenever you choose.

**Design rule [CANON]:** Strange creatures need ecology and culture, not just combat stats. Ask: What do they eat? Where do they live? Are they intelligent? Do people trade with them, protect them, or live alongside them? How do castles treat them? Do they have customs?

A tag in a heading, or in the line that introduces a list, applies to every untagged bullet under it.

## Great creatures

### Dragons [CANON]
- Deeply tied to Verdant knightly tradition.
- Most dragons are now gone or much rarer than they once were.
- Young dragons may be confused and need guidance.
- The finest knights can become dragon knights, raising dragons from hatchlings over years. See [03-knights-and-orders.md](03-knights-and-orders.md).
- Great dragons have befriended distinguished knights and allied with kingdoms and castles.
- Dragons live long enough to remember human companions for centuries after they die.
- **Dragons love chess.** Older dragons play on enormous boards carved into the floor, with human-sized pieces. This gives them an intellectual and social side beyond being giant combat animals. It isn't just a joke. It implies intelligence, patience, long memory and a strategic culture.
- Dragons are intelligent beings, not mounts.
- Open: why their numbers declined, where they went, and why freeing the eggs made them return.
- The First Quest ends with dragons sensing freed eggs and starting to return. See [08-characters-and-stories.md](08-characters-and-stories.md).

### Mountain fire dragons [CANON]
- Very powerful fire dragons live in mountains. Ordinary knights can't simply hunt one.
- **[FLAVOR, from ref 24]** They hunt alone. You "banish" one rather than kill it, and only with a legendary Verdant Knight, preferably one who speaks frost.
- **[PROVISIONAL]** A legendary Verdant Knight with frost magic confronting one. This detail came from scene development and should stay flexible.

### Cloud storm wyrms [CANON]
- Massive wyrms live among storms and clouds. It's not a single storm dragon but **a nest**. Lightning inside certain storms may literally be enormous wyrms moving. The sky has its own ecosystem of giant creatures.
- **[FLAVOR, from ref 26]** Wyrms in nests can be **multi-headed**. Every lightning bolt is one of them turning over. If you see more than one head, you're already too close.
- **[FLAVOR, from ref 04]** A storm wyrm can grow so huge it loops around a whole kingdom, and history there is divided into before it came and after it left.

### Skystorm Dragons [FLAVOR, from ref 37]
- When dark clouds start shaping into a dragon's head, a **mature Skystorm Dragon** is coming, and you can't outrun it.
- **[PROPOSED FIX]** A Skystorm Dragon is what a storm wyrm becomes when fully mature, or the apex of the storm ecosystem.

### Sky whales [FLAVOR, from ref 27]
- Enormous whales that swim through the sky, shown over a lantern-lit domed city by the sea.

### Sky jellyfish [FLAVOR, from ref 05]
- Giant jellyfish hanging in the sky. Don't touch their light: one spark throws you into a parallel realm for an unknown length of time.
- **[PROPOSED FIX]** That realm could be the near-frozen dimension of the parallel-dimension order, which would explain "for who-knows-how-long".

### Castle eagles [FLAVOR, from ref 09]
- Huge eagles that live at castles and are very protective of children. Nobles use them as school transport: they grab a child by the helmet and drop them at the academy gate.

### Unicorns [CANON]
- They exist in the Reach. Magical unicorns traditionally belong to the nobility of the Verdant Knights. They are prestigious and meaningful, possibly intelligent and judgemental, and never decorative livestock. See [03-knights-and-orders.md](03-knights-and-orders.md).

### Phoenixes [FLAVOR, strong]
- Phoenix chicks start out almost useless.
- They become formidable only after their first rebirth, after which they learn something like fire-bending.
- Adults are extremely rare, powerful and hard to win the loyalty of.
- Grown phoenixes may follow only legendary Verdant Knights, and once one chooses a knight it never leaves their side (ref 41).
- **[FLAVOR, from ref 25]** A mature phoenix is almost unbearable to stand near because of the heat. If it's calm, walk away. If it ever gets angry, you won't survive its first scream.
- The inversion: an unimpressive chick is actually an extraordinary future companion.

### Colossal ancient treefolk [CANON]
- Ancient colossal tree beings, sometimes tied to the roots of world-sized ancient trees. They live for extraordinary lengths of time. They aren't just ordinary treants made bigger.
- **[PROVISIONAL]** They **grow knights over centuries**. It is unexplained but highly distinctive, so keep it.
- **[FLAVOR, from ref 07]** Some roots of the world tree woke up and kept growing until they became mountain-sized treefolk. One sigh from them can redirect a river, and **one word can rewrite a bloodline**.
- **[PROPOSED FIX]** Rewriting bloodlines is how treefolk "grow" knights: over centuries they shape families until knights come out of them.

### Cloud-kingdom beings [PROVISIONAL]
- Angel-like beings who come down from cloud realms, some specifically looking for Verdant Knights as companions or allies.
- **[FLAVOR, from ref 13]** They're called **cloudborn heralds**, and the one shown is female. One arriving means something is about to end or begin, and the first thing she does is look for a Verdant Knight. You used this name in your own content, so it's no longer just an assistant suggestion.

### Cloud beasts [FLAVOR]
- Drawn to enormous displays of power. If you use enough power to attract one, it may identify you as the source.

### Skeleton Colossus [FLAVOR]
- Gigantic skeletal beings that work as a danger signal. Seeing one means you're in an exceptionally dangerous region. Their origin is unknown.

### Void octopus [FLAVOR]
- A floating octopus associated with the void. Looking into its eyes can reveal glimpses of the future while damaging the observer's sanity (exact effects provisional). The madness isn't necessarily immediate or total. It's almost casually dangerous.

### Primordial beasts (mythic saga) [PROVISIONAL]
- Titans, leviathans, giant serpents, huge tortoises, crystal-backed beasts, sleeping forest giants. See [09-mythic-saga.md](09-mythic-saga.md).

### Giants [CANON]
- Ancient giants can be glimpsed through the Cavern of Giants. Giant-related ruins are Elder Frontiers. What the giants are is open.

## Companions and mounts

### Moon-hounds ("good boys") [FLAVOR]
- Dog-like magical creatures whose strength grows through praise, treats, care and affection.
- A droplet of mana makes one grow overnight and wake up knowing tiny spells (ref 43).
- The principle is very Verdant: not every power is unlocked through suffering or combat. Some creatures grow stronger because someone treats them well.

### Striped war-steeds [FLAVOR]
- Unusual war horses that remember roads, choose their riders and prefer restless, adventurous people. A mount with agency, not a piece of equipment.

### Rabbitfolk [FLAVOR, from ref 29]
- A rabbit people. **Rabbitfolk messengers** carrying baskets are trusted couriers, and if one is sent, the news is serious.
- **[PROPOSED FIX]** This is probably separate from "rabbitships" (below), unless "rabbitships" was a garbled name for them.

### Rabbitships [FLAVOR]
- A strange creature or concept. Groups or flocks attach themselves to people and effectively adopt them as shepherds. Their biology is undefined. It's highly distinctive, so keep it.

### Giant toad [CANON]
- Enormous toads in some swamps carry travellers, knights, boats and cargo. See [06-places.md](06-places.md).

### Rain spirits [FLAVOR, strong]
- They appear around people who are sad and outdoors in the rain. They don't try to fix the problem. They just keep the person company, so you don't cry alone (ref 31). Shown as a vast, gentle face formed from the rain. One of the most emotionally distinctive Verdant creatures.

### Giant owls [FLAVOR]
- Advisors, not warriors. Valued for wisdom, memory and observation.

## Small folk and everyday creatures

### Stonefolk [FLAVOR, recurring]
- One of the more recurring non-human peoples. They can be tiny.
- Social custom: travellers tip them pebbles.
- Small, intelligent and generally peaceful with travellers.
- Young Stonefolk dislike heavy rain because water erosion damages their outer layers. Every downpour sands **a year** off a stone child's surface, and the kind thing to do is hold an umbrella over them (ref 17). So their relationship with weather is physically different from that of organic creatures.
- **[ARCHIVE]** Glowing blue slit eyes and orbiting pebbles were prompt additions by the assistant.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) Your studio notes split the stone peoples into **pebble folk** and **stone folk**. Both are yours. How they fit the Stonefolk above is not settled: see S6 in [10-contradictions-and-fixes.md](10-contradictions-and-fixes.md). Both versions are kept.

### Pebble folk [CANON, studio lore]

- **[CANON]** (studio lore) They learned a few words of human tongue to say it isn't theirs: **"he is not ours"**.
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Pebble folk live on the Still Water shore. See [06-places.md](06-places.md).
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Pebble folk the loaf rolled over are stuck in its crust. Flour it and you see dark lumps, some with their eyes still open.
- **[GAME TEXT]** (Muster lore line) "Roll to travel. Blamed for the Loaf's tracks, so they learned two human words."
- **[GAME TEXT]** (Muster, Kolobok) Pebble tap lines: "not ours not ours not ours", "not ours!", "not ours, not ours". They say it as a run of high clicks and hop in place while they chatter.
- **[GAME TEXT]** (Field Tip 6) "If the pebbles on a lakeshore blink at you, don't skip them across the water. They remember."

Their eyes glow mint green and blink. That is a studio art convention, not lore (compare the archived blue slit eyes above, and S15).

### The two words
- **[CANON]** (studio lore, your wording) The phrase is "he is not ours", described as "a few words of human tongue".
- **[GAME TEXT]** Every game compresses it to two words, **NOT OURS**; the Muster says "two human words" and Field Tip 2 says "It knows two words". Treat the two-word form as the games' script and "he is not ours" as what the folk actually learned. See S14 in [10-contradictions-and-fixes.md](10-contradictions-and-fixes.md).

### Mud / garden golems [FLAVOR]
- Surprisingly gentle. These mossy, mud-covered giants love flowers, tend plants and quietly bring a bouquet **to people who have been kind to the forest** (ref 42).

### Pocket spiders [FLAVOR]
- Tiny spiders that tell jokes.

### Snail spiders [FLAVOR]
- Harmless hybrids that can barely cause trouble but are extraordinarily hard to harm ("maxed defense").

### Ghost innkeepers [FLAVOR]
- Ghosts who run inns and keep serving coffee, tea and travellers. In parts of the Reach, undeath is part of ordinary life, not automatically horror.

### Treants [FLAVOR]
- Smaller tree beings, separate from the colossal treefolk. Running gag: treants arm-wrestle.

### Castle gargoyles [FLAVOR]
- Domesticated in some castles as guardians, companions and traditional castle creatures, not invading monsters.

### Lizards [CANON]
- **[CANON]** Intelligent lizard peoples exist.
- **[FLAVOR]** Some of them simply enjoy yoga. Deliberately mundane world texture.

### The Lizard Archivist [FLAVOR, recurring]
- A formidable lizard who guards dangerous or forbidden books (ref 32 shows a huge, monstrous lizard bursting through the stacks). He is not a comic librarian, and intruders should take him seriously. If he spots you, the expected response is to run. His identity is undeveloped. The exact title (librarian, bookkeeper, archivist) shifted.

### Goblin kings [FLAVOR]
- Rule: whoever physically holds and wears the goblin crown is king. Built-in political chaos. This is a local goblin custom, not something every goblin society does unless deliberately established.

### Shroom knights [FLAVOR]
- Real non-human mushroom people, not humans in mushroom armour. After rain their numbers multiply dramatically, so an army seems to keep rising out of the ground.

### Mana snowmen [FLAVOR]
- Snow beings created by mana. They're friendly, and problems start only when someone brings fire (ref 22). What happens then is undefined.

### Lighthouse crabs [FLAVOR, from ref 15]
- Crabs that live around teleport lighthouses. The lighthouses are safest when the crabs are away. See [06-places.md](06-places.md).

### Sentient / possessive helmets [FLAVOR]
- Certain helmets react to potential owners, softly clanging something like "mine." Whether that's speech, enchantment or a spirit is unknown.

### Mana-blue creatures [FLAVOR]
- Creatures saturated with mana can turn visibly blue, which works as an in-world sign of magical saturation.

## Peoples and society

### Peoples [CANON]
- At minimum: humans, elves, dwarves, dragonkin, goblins, lizard peoples, giants and half-giants, Stonefolk, and mushroom peoples. Others may exist.
- Don't assume any people has a single unified nation. Species and cultures mix throughout the setting.
- Unusual species also live ordinary lives and work ordinary jobs.

### Collectors, the reborn Librarians [CANON, studio lore]
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) Collectors are reborn Librarians, following visions toward a Library that vanished in the Old Age. Full entry: [05-ancient-history.md](05-ancient-history.md).
- **[GAME TEXT]** (Muster) **The Collector**, home "The ruins". Lore line: "A Librarian reborn. Follows visions toward a Library that no longer exists, and finds everything else." (Roster table: "A Librarian reborn, following visions toward a Library that no longer exists.") Tap lines: "Do it all in one life." "Not this door either."
- **[GAME TEXT]** Drawn as a hunched, hooded figure with a staff and a lantern beside a ruined arch that opens onto stars that should not be there. The same lantern figure stands by the lone door on Field Tip 4.
- **[PROVISIONAL]** Note: the roster credits "the Collectors" (the guild) to you. A single Collector as a roster creature is the studio's framing.

### Occupations and archetypes [CANON]
- Mages, paladins, clerics, rangers, necromancers, assassins, rogues, fighters, cultists, artificers and dragon knights. These are cultural or occupational categories, not game classes.

### Dragonkin [FLAVOR]
- Humans and dragonkin can form ordinary families. Example: a dragonkin-human family that runs a bakery. Peoples coexist instead of each species being a geopolitical monoculture.

### Half-giants [FLAVOR]
- Some work as traders and merchants.

### Cooks and forbidden mushrooms [FLAVOR]
- Some cooks secretly use forbidden mushrooms. Why they're forbidden is open: magical effects, hallucinations, dangerous biology, a legal monopoly, religious taboo, extreme flavour or addiction. Don't decide without deliberate development.

## Creatures of the roads, Still Water and the covenant (studio lore)

These come from the studio pieces: Kolobok, the Muster of the Reach, the Reach Field Tips, Still Water and Hero of the Reach. Studio lore marked [his] is **[CANON]**. Roster tiers, stats, counts and "Available" numbers are placeholders a previous Claude made up and are never lore.

### Kolobok, the invisible loaf [CANON premise, studio lore]
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) A huge, invisible being inspired by Kolobok, the runaway round bun of the folk tale. It terrorises roads and fields.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) It only becomes visible if you blow a lot of flour on it.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) Pebble people and stone people hate it, because humans kept attacking them thinking its damage was theirs. See the stone peoples above.
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Names: road folk call it "the Loaf"; stone folk call it "Soft". Whether "Kolobok" is an in-world name is open.
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Origin: a famine winter. An old woman scraped the bins and floor cracks for the last flour and baked one round loaf. Years before she had caught the talking fish at Still Water and saved her wish. She wished "Let no one ever take this bread from us." Granted word for word: no one can take it, catch it, eat it, or see it. It rolled off the windowsill while cooling and never stopped, including away from her.
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Why it works in the Reach: magic doesn't work there, but a wish isn't a spell, so there is nothing to dispel. (Rests on the studio's "magic doesn't work" wording; see S2.)
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Whatever it rolls over sticks to the dough. It is hill-sized and still growing. It isn't hunting: like the tale, it is always running away, and at that size running away flattens the road.
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Its song is a muffled boast listing everyone it rolled away from. Hear your village in the verse and it has already been there.
- **[GAME TEXT]** (Kolobok) The song: "I rolled from the bin and I rolled from the sill, / I rolled from the miller, I rolled from the mill, / I rolled from the knights and the stones on the hill, / and I'll roll from you. I am rolling still." After it is fed: "I rolled from your flour, and I'm hungry still." Heard muffled, as if through bread, louder as it nears.
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Signs: round flattened swaths through wheat overnight; warm bread smell on an empty road (go uphill, it's slow uphill); crows standing on nothing, walking in place on its crust; foxes trailing it ("Every loaf meets its fox").
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Only a lot of flour shows it, as a white hill rolling for a few breaths. Then the dough drinks the flour. Every sack you throw to see it makes it bigger.
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Road wardens hang "flour bells" (sacks on poles) over roads. See [06-places.md](06-places.md).
- **[GAME TEXT]** (Muster) Home "The roads". Lore line: "No one can take it. No one can see it. Flour shows it, and flour feeds it." Growth "+1 size a sack".
- **[GAME TEXT]** (Kolobok captions) "Warm bread on the air. Nothing on the road." "The crows are standing on nothing." "The flour bell burst." "There it is." "It drinks the flour." "It's bigger now." "Every sack makes it bigger." "Flour on the wheat. Nothing under it." "That was the last sack." "No flour left in the village." "It takes the road." Prompts: "Tap it to sling flour", "Aim where the crows stand".
- **[GAME TEXT]** (Kolobok, depicted) A sphere about 15 m in radius before anyone feeds it, with three baker's slashes across its top, seven lumps in the crust (pebble folk it rolled over) and five crows riding it. Flour clings to its skin and fades in patches as the dough drinks it. It casts a long shadow only where flour covers it. It presses a round track into waist-high wheat. The village has nine sacks of flour to throw.
- **[GAME TEXT]** (Kolobok endings) Three endings. "Not ours" and "Seen" are quoted under Stone folk and stone elders above. "Fed": "You saw it every time, and every time it drank the flour. It left singing a new verse. The verse is about you."
- **[GAME TEXT]** (Field Tip 1) "If the road smells of warm bread and nothing's on it, get uphill. It's slow uphill." (Field Tip 5) "If a flour bell bursts and nobody's near it, don't stay to see what's there." Card 5 shows flour clinging to something huge, a white hill on the road, patchy where it has already drunk the flour, crows on top, a snapped pole and a torn sack, the traveller already leaving.
- **[GAME TEXT]** (Hero of the Reach, Flour Sack) "For the roads at harvest. Throw it hard, and don't stay to watch." Implies knights carry flour against the loaf at harvest.
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Hook: the knights won their covenant with wordplay; the fox in the tale won with flattery. Stopping the loaf may be a talking job, not a fight.
