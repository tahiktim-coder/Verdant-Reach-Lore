# Ancient History

The world is older and stranger than its current civilizations realize. Ancient machines still run, and whole landscapes may be wounds left by history.

## The three ancient eras (studio lore)

Your studio notes divide "ancient" into levels. This is the first dated spine the world has had. How it lines up with the mythic saga and with the arcane-mech civilization below is not settled; see [09-mythic-saga.md](09-mythic-saga.md) and the era-mapping contradiction in [10-contradictions-and-fixes.md](10-contradictions-and-fixes.md).

- **[CANON]** (studio lore) "Ancient" has two levels in your terms, and a third era follows them:
  1. **The god-war era** ("level 2 ancient"). Mythological-tier beings fought. "Gods" here means godlike to humans, not universe creators. The cursed sword and the rally scroll both come from this era, "by coincidence from the same story".
  2. **The Old Age** ("level 1 ancient"). Home of the cursed warrior's legend and of the Librarians.
  3. **The New Age.** "Also ancient now. Old Age legends were already ancient during the New Age."
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) The present comes after all three: the New Age is "also ancient now".
- **[PROPOSED FIX]** Your notes do not date the Verdant Knights' covenant, Kolobok or Still Water. The studio brief calls them your current projects and the games play them as the present (Sir Aldren: "The Hunter came twenty years ago"). Treat them as present-day material until you say otherwise.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) What the legends below establish about each era:
  - God-war era: an ancient mage made a rally spell "to bring allies to his other allies fighting gods", so mortals and mages took part in the war of gods. A colossal summit in a parallel dimension of Old magic was "the site of an ancient godly battle". The cursed sword was made.
  - Old Age: kings and realms, a capital reached by one mountain pass, armies of swarms, mages, creatures and archmages. A minor godlike being, the "dirty deceiver", still walked the world. Warriors wore gear that channelled teleportation, projection, telekinesis, minor future sight and a danger reflex. Kings commissioned the reincarnating Librarians to unveil secrets of late-stage ancient magic. The Library vanished.
  - New Age: Verdant Reach knights of some castle already existed and fought a necromancer who had researched Old Age gear.
- **[GAME TEXT]** (from The Summit Road, title screen) "A legend from before the Old Age." Your notes place the cursed warrior's legend in the Old Age and only the scroll and the sword before it; see the contradictions file.
- **[PROPOSED FIX]** In short (the full table is in [09-mythic-saga.md](09-mythic-saga.md)): god-war era = the First Light and Branch A (Beasts, Depths, Fall of Titans); Old Age = the First Kingdoms through the Age of Artifice, which would make the arcane-mech civilization an Old Age power; New Age = the Voice of Iron through the Memory Eclipse; present = the Age of Quiet Wonder. Not yours; awaiting your decision.

## The ancient arcane-mech civilization

- **[CANON]** An older civilization built extraordinary things, and the modern mage society of the South overthrew it. See [04-the-wider-world.md](04-the-wider-world.md).
- **[CANON]** Capabilities developed for it:
  - colossal machines
  - mechs that can move mountains
  - bio-mechanical dragons
  - floating cities and floating bastions
  - magical megastructures
  - sky-chains
  - dragon-engines
  - vast mechanical infrastructure
  - barriers and seals
  - systems that may still run long after their builders vanished
- **[CANON]** **Some ancient structures aren't ruins at all. They are still doing their job.** Modern people just don't know what that job is anymore. An ancient machine might be holding something underground, maintaining a barrier, preventing a catastrophe, or regulating something no current civilization understands.
- Open: what caused its deeper fall beyond the mages' overthrow, and whether it is the same civilization as the mythic Age of Artifice or the builders of the war machines in the Silent Battlefield.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) The Librarians of the Old Age preserved "ancient secrets, magic and technology", and the Engineers guild that descends from them practises "magic engineering, constructs". So the Old Age had technology worth preserving and a tradition that kept building constructs afterward. Whether that technology is this civilization's is open; see the era mapping in [09-mythic-saga.md](09-mythic-saga.md).

## Magic versus ancient machinery

- **[PROVISIONAL]** In the earliest worldbuilding, ancient machinery was highly resistant or almost immune to magic. This created the basic North/South tension:
  - The South trusts magic.
  - The North has lived alongside ancient technology.
  - Ancient machines may overturn the assumptions of mage societies.
- **[PROVISIONAL]** **The machine under the mountain.** An early major event: a gigantic machine was discovered beneath a southern mountain and awakened. The discovery proved that the North's supposedly primitive relationship with machinery was connected to a much larger forgotten civilization. The southern powers considered hiding the discovery and investigating the North. This is older lore, but it fits the later arcane-mech history very well.

## Old Age legend: the cursed sword and the last stand

Your own legend, from the studio lore, so it is canon as a legend the world tells. Your notes separate what people believed the scroll did from what it really does, and that split is kept below. The magic rules it implies (Old magic, the scroll, the curse) are in [02-magic.md](02-magic.md).

- **[CANON]** (studio lore) A "dirty deceiver" (a minor godlike being) gifted a cursed blade to a king's son: a great warrior, rumoured about 5% divine in lineage.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) His realm was attacked from all sides. One mountain pass led to the capital. Odds of 1000 to 1.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) The sword compels its wielder to kill everyone. He spent his will resisting it until he reached the pass alone, then held it for months against swarms, mages, creatures and archmages, until the corpse piles and stench made the enemy withdraw.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) With his last will he used a rare legendary teleport scroll, so he couldn't hurt anyone while the blade wouldn't let go.
- **[LEGEND]** (studio lore) What the scroll was believed to do: send you extremely far in an unpredictable direction outside the known world.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) What the scroll really does: it is an improvised rally spell from before the Old Age, made by an ancient mage to bring allies to his other allies fighting gods. It sends you to a parallel dimension of Old magic: a near-infinite, symmetrical, gradually sloped walkable mountain. You arrive above stormy magical clouds with the colossal summit, the site of an ancient godly battle, visible. Its purpose was lost; it got reused as an escape.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) The sword won't let its wielder die. It weakens him until it takes over ("held by the sword"). He is still in that empty dimension, walking toward the summit for centuries or millennia.
- **[PROVISIONAL]** (studio lore, working) The summit is visible thousands of km away, marked by green lightning or similar.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) The cursed sword and the rally scroll both come from the god-war era, "by coincidence from the same story". The gifting of the sword is Old Age; the sword itself is older.

## Old Age: the Librarians and the Library that vanished

Your lore. This is the full entry; 03, 04, 07 and 08 summarise it. The three guilds that grew out of the Librarians are present-day institutions and belong with the orders in [03-knights-and-orders.md](03-knights-and-orders.md) or [04-the-wider-world.md](04-the-wider-world.md); the ancient part is here, with the guilds summarised so nothing is lost.

- **[CANON]** (studio lore) The Librarians of the Old Age: reincarnating preservers of ancient secrets, magic and technology. Kings and rulers commissioned them to unveil specific secrets of magic, knowing each discovery took 4 to 5 reincarnations, so the requester was usually long dead by delivery. The secrets were late-stage ancient magic.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) Still in the Old Age, the Library suddenly and magically disappeared. Reincarnated librarians could no longer reach it or remember it but kept their calling, so they founded an order and kept digging for ancient knowledge from sources never in the Library.
- **[PROVISIONAL]** (studio lore, working) The order's name is undecided; you want something that fits both genders.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) Over thousands of years the order became three guilds, each with its own capital today:
  - **Mages:** experimental decipherers without the true sources. One broad and one narrow field each; the narrow one is now often a branch of dark magic they never practised before.
  - **Engineers:** magic engineering, constructs.
  - **Collectors** (the most interesting, in your words): across reincarnations they drift toward adventurous lives, following visions and intuition from something they feel they know deep down, believing to the end it will lead them back to the vanished Library. Instead it leads them to uncover mysteries. They bring finds to the Engineers and decipher them together, but don't work with the Mages.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) Collectors' credo: don't rely on next lives, do everything in one. Don't resume where a past life left off. Once mature enough to realise they're reincarnated, they're at peace with it: a new life is a new life.
- **[GAME TEXT]** (from Muster of the Reach, The Collector, home "The ruins") "A Librarian reborn. Follows visions toward a Library that no longer exists, and finds everything else." Tap lines: "Do it all in one life." "Not this door either." The portrait, "an arch that opens onto nothing": two worn pillars and a keystone with "stars that shouldn't be there" showing through the opening, and a small figure with a staff and a lantern.
- **[GAME TEXT]** (from Reach Field Tips, card 4, "a door standing alone in a field") "If you find a door standing alone in a field, look through it. Don't walk through it. The Library isn't there anymore." The card shows a worn stone arch in a moonlit night field. Through it: "a golden afternoon from somewhere else", a strip of wheat, "a small cloud inside the doorway, lit by a sun you can't see", and motes drifting out of the doorway. A figure with a lantern (the same Collector sprite as the Muster) stands beside it.
- **[PROPOSED FIX]** The two game pieces imply that doors or arches once led to the Library and now open onto somewhere else. Your notes say only that the Library disappeared and can neither be reached nor remembered. Treat the doors as studio elaboration until you confirm them. If kept, they sit well with this bible's "places where the rules break" and with the Cavern of Giants, where an opening shows the distant past.
- Open: what the Library was, where it stood, who or what removed it, whether the Reach's own castle libraries hold any Librarian sources, and whether the Library is the Luminous Archive of the visual saga. See [09-mythic-saga.md](09-mythic-saga.md) and [11-open-questions.md](11-open-questions.md).

## New Age legend: the necromancer and the projection

Your lore, and the one studio legend with Verdant Reach knights in it.

- **[CANON]** (studio lore) Verdant Reach knights from some castle fought a necromancer who summoned the cursed warrior of the Old Age legend, wielding the sword. Where the fight happened, outside the Reach or in a cave inside it, is deliberately left unknown.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) The necromancer summoned a projection, not a dead spirit. He researched what Old Age warriors wore to channel and contain their energy and found teachings to project a fraction of the wearer. The gear and the numbers are in [02-magic.md](02-magic.md).
- **[PROVISIONAL]** (studio lore, working) The projection holds about 1/1000 of the warrior's power (weighing 1/10000): deadly to strong fighters, not army-destroying.
- **[GAME TEXT]** (from Muster of the Reach, The Projection, home "A necromancer's circle") "Not a spirit: a thousandth of the cursed warrior, pulled through a circle. Only the sword is solid." Tap lines: "It does not know it is a copy." "The sword still wants everyone." The portrait: inside a cave, a glowing green circle on the floor with six points orbiting it, the necromancer small and hunched with arms raised, the projection a flickering translucent figure whose sword is "the only solid thing".
- **[PROPOSED FIX]** A code detail, not lore: the projection is drawn with the Verdant Knight's sprite at double scale. Do not read that as the cursed warrior being a Verdant Knight. Your notes make him a king's son of the Old Age, and the Summit Road never calls him a knight.
- Open: whether the rite can be repeated, and whether projecting him touches the warrior on the summit. See [11-open-questions.md](11-open-questions.md).
