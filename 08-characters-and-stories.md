# Characters and Stories

## The Red-Caped Knight

- **[CANON]** The central recurring protagonist and a Verdant Knight.
- **[CANON]** Appearance: usually in full armour, often with a closed helmet, a distinctive red cape or cloak, and usually a sword or greatsword.
- **[CANON]** **He is NOT the last Verdant Knight.** He is one knight among many, and the story simply follows him. The Reach is a functioning civilization, not a "last surviving order" cliché.
- **[CANON]** His first quest was the dragon-egg legend (below).
- **[PROVISIONAL]** Scenes from his training grounds were developed.
- Open: whether he is the same figure who appears across the mythic ages. See [10-contradictions-and-fixes.md](10-contradictions-and-fixes.md).
- **[GAME TEXT]** (from Reach Field Tips) The traveller on nine of the 13 cards (1, 2, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13) is a small figure in a steel helm and greaves with a red cape. On card 13 he is called a knight and stands on a castle wall counting his years. This is the red-caped knight as the studio draws him. The Muster's Verdant Knight stands at dawn on a hill with a far castle, cape blown left, carrying a banner pole and pennant, with light showing through the visor. Sir Aldren in Hero of the Reach wears a green cape and the Order's leaf. See S13.
- Open: whether the red-caped knight belongs to the covenant order at all, and whether the Hunter has ever come for him. See [11-open-questions.md](11-open-questions.md).

## The Female Knight / Blonde Companion

- **[PROVISIONAL]** Later adventure material repeatedly paired the red-caped knight with a female companion. She is usually blonde and adventurous, sometimes explicitly another knight, and she travels beside him as an equal, not as someone to rescue.
- **[FLAVOR]** She appears in many of the more intimate and whimsical encounters, including one where she negotiates angrily with tiny elves.
- Open: her name and permanent identity are not established.

## The First Quest: The Stolen Dragon Eggs

**[CANON]** This is one of the clearest real stories in the world.

**Backstory.** Centuries earlier, a knight of the order stole dragon eggs. He found that eating them kept him young and delayed ageing. Eventually it drove him mad. The tale survived.

**Sequence.**
1. The tale is told to the red-caped knight when he is young.
2. Years later, he chooses it as his first quest.
3. He searches for the stolen eggs.
4. He discovers ancient ruins.
5. The eggs are hidden inside an enormous egg-shaped stone sculpture.
6. The ancient, corrupted Verdant Knight is still there.
7. They duel.
8. The young knight defeats him.
9. The eggs are freed.
10. Dragons sense the eggs have been released and begin returning.

**Meaning.**
- **[CANON]** The antagonist's unnatural survival from eating dragon eggs is part of what drove him mad.
- **[CANON]** The hero isn't saving the last dragons in existence. The dragons' return gives the quest a larger consequence.
- **[CANON]** It shows that Verdant institutions have their own shameful or dangerous historical figures.

## The Cursed Sword and the Last Stand (Old Age legend)

- **[CANON]** (studio lore) A “dirty deceiver”, a minor godlike being, gifted a cursed blade to a king's son: a great warrior, rumoured about **5% divine** in lineage.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) His realm was attacked from all sides. One mountain pass led to the capital. The sword compels its wielder to kill everyone. He spent his will resisting it until he reached the pass alone, then held it for **months** until the enemy withdrew.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) With his last will he used a rare legendary **teleport scroll**, believed to send you extremely far in an unpredictable direction outside the known world, so he could not hurt anyone while the blade would not let go (the belief itself is [LEGEND]; what the scroll really does is in [05-ancient-history.md](05-ancient-history.md)).
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) The sword will not let its wielder die. It weakens him until it takes over (“held by the sword”). He is still in the empty dimension the scroll sent him to, walking toward the summit for centuries or millennia.
- **[GAME TEXT]** (from The Summit Road) The vignette calls him “the cursed warrior” and never “knight”. Subtitle: “A legend from before the Old Age”. Two of its six looping captions: “He read the scroll so the sword could hurt no one.” “He has been walking for a thousand years.” (all six are in [17-studio-and-games.md](17-studio-and-games.md)) It shows a paved slab road along a terraced ridge, colossal swords planted point-down along it (“relics: colossal blades left from the war of gods”), a cloud sea, storm banks, green motes, and a green-glowing sword dragging and sparking behind a cloaked walker seen from behind. The summit is fixed on screen and never gets closer. The “thousand years” and “before the Old Age” lines are game copy. See S9.
- **[PROPOSED FIX]** Relation to the First Quest villain: both are warriors kept alive unnaturally, one by dragon eggs, one by a blade. The Order's ban on cursed blades (Hero of the Reach) reads as the lesson the Reach drew from this legend. Suggestion only.

Full legend: [05-ancient-history.md](05-ancient-history.md).

## The Necromancer and the Projection (New Age legend)

- **[CANON]** (studio lore) Verdant Reach knights from some castle fought a necromancer who summoned a projection of the cursed warrior wielding the sword from the Old Age legend. Where it happened, outside the Reach or in a cave inside it, is deliberately unknown.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) The necromancer summons a **projection**, not a dead spirit. He researched what Old Age warriors wore to channel and contain their energy (teleportation, projection, telekinesis, minor future sight, danger reflex) and found teachings to project a fraction of the wearer. **[PROVISIONAL]** (working) The worn things may be bracelets or something, undecided.
- **[PROVISIONAL]** (studio lore, working) The projection holds about **1/1000 of the warrior's power**, weighing 1/10000: deadly to strong fighters, not army-destroying.
- **[GAME TEXT]** (from the Muster) “The Projection”, home “A necromancer’s circle”. Lore line: “Not a spirit: a thousandth of the cursed warrior, pulled through a circle. Only the sword is solid.” Tap lines: “It does not know it is a copy.” and “The sword still wants everyone.” Drawn as a flickering dithered figure framed by a cave mouth over a glowing circle on the floor, the necromancer small and hunched with arms raised, and the sword the only solid thing. The figure reuses the Verdant Knight's sprite at double size, a code shortcut, not a statement that the warrior was a Verdant Knight.

## Zidion

- **[CANON]** One of the few named minor characters. A travelling merchant who can teleport. He turns up with supplies in remote places where normal trade routes would be impossible.
- **[FLAVOR, from ref 06]** Zidion is also a **shop**: a roadside stall with "ZIDION" on its sign that appears where roads end, "the shop you find when every sane store is miles behind". So perhaps the shop itself appears, rather than Zidion walking up with a pack.
