## Map

- **[his]** The western plain is the **Verdant Reach**. Magic doesn't work there. Each castle is unique.
- **[his]** In the east lies one big kingdom full of mages (see "The eastern kingdom").
- **[his]** In the north, a mountain path leads to an unknown sea and a strip of land.
- **[his]** The Verdant Reach and Still Water can be the same universe (so can Kolobok).

## Eras (oldest first)

"Ancient" has two levels in his terms:

1. **The god-war era** ("level 2 ancient"). Mythological-tier beings fought; "gods" here means godlike to
   humans, not universe creators. The cursed sword and the rally scroll both come from this era, by
   coincidence from the same story.
2. **The Old Age** ("level 1 ancient"). Home of the cursed warrior's legend and of the Librarians.
3. **The New Age.** Also ancient now. Old Age legends were already ancient during the New Age.

## The cursed sword and the last stand (Old Age legend) [his]

- A "dirty deceiver" (a minor godlike being) gifted a cursed blade to a king's son: a great warrior, rumoured
  about 5% divine in lineage.
- His realm was attacked from all sides; one mountain pass led to the capital; odds of 1000 to 1.
- The sword compels its wielder to kill everyone. He spent his will resisting it until he reached the pass
  alone, then held it for months against swarms, mages, creatures and archmages, until the corpse piles and
  stench made the enemy withdraw.
- With his last will he used a rare legendary teleport scroll, believed to send you extremely far in an
  unpredictable direction outside the known world, so he couldn't hurt anyone while the blade wouldn't let go.
- **What the scroll really does:** it is an improvised rally spell from before the Old Age, made by an ancient
  mage to bring allies to his other allies fighting gods. It sends you to a parallel dimension of Old magic:
  a near-infinite, symmetrical, gradually sloped walkable mountain. You arrive above stormy magical clouds with
  the colossal summit (site of an ancient godly battle) visible. Its purpose was lost; it got reused as an escape.
- The sword won't let its wielder die; it weakens him until it takes over ("held by the sword"). He is still
  in that empty dimension, walking toward the summit for centuries or millennia. **[working]** The summit is
  visible thousands of km away, marked by green lightning or similar.

## The necromancer (New Age legend) [his]

- Verdant Reach knights from some castle fought a necromancer who summoned the spirit of a cursed warrior
  wielding the sword from the Old Age legend. Where the fight happened (outside the Reach, or a cave inside
  it) is deliberately left unknown.
- The necromancer summons a **projection**, not a dead spirit. He researched what Old Age warriors wore to
  channel and contain their energy (teleportation, projection, telekinesis, minor future sight, danger reflex;
  **[working]** bracelets or something, undecided) and found teachings to project a fraction of the wearer.
- **[working]** The projection holds about 1/1000 of the warrior's power (weighing 1/10000): deadly to strong
  fighters, not army-destroying.

## The Verdant Knights and the covenant [his]

- One castle houses the **Verdant Knights** order.
- On one of their epic quests the order escaped an inescapable death. Since then they are bound by a covenant.
- **[working]** Origin: the knights used wordplay when entering the covenant during a quest they were nearly
  losing, and it worked; that's why things now work this way.
- A creature manifests unpredictably to kill a targeted knight with its "death weapon". No one can predict when.
- From the creature's side it is one continuous fight: it fights, blinks into a parallel dimension for a few
  seconds, then resumes, while for the knight 20 years or more may have passed. No one knows how it works.
- If anything else tries to kill the knight, the creature stops it. It seems vital to the creature that IT
  lands the finishing blow. Its motives are otherwise unknown.
- He treats this as a concept to keep and refine later.

## The Librarians and the order they became [his]

- **The Librarians (Old Age):** reincarnating preservers of ancient secrets, magic and technology. Kings and
  rulers commissioned them to unveil specific secrets of magic, knowing each discovery took 4–5 reincarnations,
  so the requester was usually long dead by delivery. The secrets were late-stage ancient magic.
- Still in the Old Age, the Library suddenly and magically disappeared. Reincarnated librarians could no longer
  reach it or remember it but kept their calling, so they founded an order (**[working]** name undecided; he
  wants something that fits both genders) and kept digging for ancient knowledge from sources never in the Library.
- Over thousands of years the order became three guilds, each with its own capital today:
  - **Mages:** experimental decipherers without the true sources. One broad and one narrow field each; the
    narrow one is now often a branch of dark magic they never practised before.
  - **Engineers:** magic engineering, constructs.
  - **Collectors** (the most interesting): across reincarnations they drift toward adventurous lives, following
    visions and intuition from something they feel they know deep down, believing to the end it will lead them
    back to the vanished Library. Instead it leads them to uncover mysteries. They bring finds to the engineers
    and decipher them together, but don't work with the mages.
- **Collectors' credo:** don't rely on next lives, do everything in one. Don't resume where a past life left
  off. Once mature enough to realise they're reincarnated, they're at peace with it: a new life is a new life.

## The eastern kingdom [his]

- One big kingdom full of mages, built so its people sin, because sinning takes away their magic.
- From outside it looks amazing: lots of food and fun. The system exists to take people further from their
  God, so minor deities can rule over their kingdoms.
- Legalised sins today: brothels, killings, a celebration day when no crime is punished, killing unwanted
  babies below a certain age, and a day of abundance where people indulge until they lose their minds.

## Kolobok and the stone folk

See `kolobok.md`. **[his]** premise: a huge, invisible, Kolobok-like being terrorises roads and fields and
only shows when a lot of flour is blown on it. Pebble people and stone people hate it, because humans kept
attacking them thinking its damage was theirs; they learned a few human words to say "he is not ours".
