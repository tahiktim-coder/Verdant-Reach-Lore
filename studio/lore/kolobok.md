# Kolobok: the invisible loaf

## His premise [his]
- A huge, invisible being inspired by Kolobok (the runaway round bun of the folk tale). It terrorises roads and fields.
- It only becomes visible if you blow a lot of flour on it.
- Pebble people and stone people hate it: humans kept attacking them, thinking its damage was their doing.
- They learned a few words of human tongue to say it isn't theirs: "he is not ours".

## Details Claude proposed [proposed, not yet confirmed]
Built into the Kolobok prototype (`.claude/skills/pixel-scenes/examples/kolobok/`). Ask before treating as canon.
- **Names.** Road folk call it "the Loaf"; stone folk call it "Soft".
- **Origin.** Famine winter; an old woman scraped the bins and floor cracks for the last flour and baked one round loaf.
  Years before she had caught the talking fish at Still Water and saved her wish. She wished: "Let no one ever take
  this bread from us." Granted word for word: no one can take it, catch it, eat it, or see it. It rolled off the
  windowsill while cooling and never stopped, including away from her.
- **Why it works in the Reach.** Magic doesn't work there, but a wish isn't a spell, so there is nothing to dispel.
- **Now.** Whatever it rolls over sticks to the dough; it's hill-sized and still growing. It isn't hunting: like the
  tale, it is always running away, and at that size running away flattens the road.
- **Its song.** A muffled boast listing everyone it rolled away from. Hear your village in the verse and it has already
  been there. Prototype verses: "I rolled from the bin and I rolled from the sill, / I rolled from the miller, I rolled
  from the mill, / I rolled from the knights and the stones on the hill, / and I'll roll from you. I am rolling still."
  After it's fed: "I rolled from your flour, and I'm hungry still."
- **Signs.** Round flattened swaths through wheat overnight; warm bread smell on an empty road (go uphill, it's slow
  uphill); crows standing on nothing, walking in place on its crust; foxes trailing it ("Every loaf meets its fox").
- **Flour.** Only a lot of flour shows it, as a white hill rolling for a few breaths. Then the dough drinks the flour:
  every sack you throw to see it makes it bigger. Road wardens hang "flour bells" (sacks on poles) over roads.
- **Stone and pebble folk.** Pebble folk live on the Still Water shore; stone folk are field and boundary stones.
  Masons were paid per stone. Their one phrase is NOT OURS: pebbles clack it, elders cut it into their own faces.
  The stone folk (natural millers) taught humans the flour trick knowing it feeds the loaf. Pebble folk it rolled over
  are stuck in its crust; flour it and you see dark lumps, some with their eyes still open.
- **Hook.** The knights won their covenant with wordplay; the fox in the tale won with flattery. Stopping it may be a
  talking job, not a fight.

## Prototype mechanics (Kolobok piece)
Nine sacks. A flour bell bursts once and shows it. Hits coat it, it casts a long shadow, crows scatter, then it drinks
the flour and grows. Tap the stone elder to hear it. Endings: Not ours (no hits), Seen (1–4), Fed (5+).
