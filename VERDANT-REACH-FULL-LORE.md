### How to read this bible

Every idea is tagged with a status so lore never gets lost or quietly changed again.

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[CANON]** | Came from a dedicated lore conversation or real story development. This is the source of truth. |
| **[PROVISIONAL]** | The idea is real and yours, but its name, details or placement are not locked yet. |
| **[FLAVOR]** | Came from TikTok/visual work. It fits the world and can be used as texture, but it does not override canon. It can be promoted to canon later. |
| **[PROPOSED FIX]** | A suggested way to resolve a contradiction or gap. Not canon until you approve it. |
| **[LEGEND]** | Believed by characters in the world, but not necessarily objectively true. |
| **[ARCHIVE]** | An assistant invention you never adopted, or an abandoned branch. Kept for reference only. |
| **[NOT CANON]** | Explicitly rejected. Never use. |
| **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** | A sub-kind of PROVISIONAL. A previous Claude in the Reach Studio proposed it (marked [proposed] in `studio/lore/*.md`) and you have not confirmed it. Keep it, say it came from the studio, never present it as yours. |
| **[GAME TEXT]** | Player-facing text co-written in the studio's games: tips, lore lines, tap lines, item descriptions, captions, endings. It is the games' actual script and usable flavor, not settled world canon unless the idea under it is marked [his] in a studio lore note. Stats, tiers and counts in the games are never lore. |

A tag in a heading, or in the line that introduces a list, applies to every untagged bullet under it.

#### Source hierarchy (highest wins)

1. Explicit lore discussions: magic, politics, history, institutions, regions.
2. Explicit story development: the First Quest, characters, real events.
3. Ideas that recurred across visual/TikTok work and clearly became part of the world.
4. One-off TikTok jokes, captions and prompts. These are flavor only until promoted. The idea underneath may be canon, but modern phrasing like RPG terms, "max defense" or "good boys" is not formal lore wording.
5. Assistant ideas you never accepted.

The reference images in [14-visual-references.md](14-visual-references.md) sit at level 3–4: a loose idea bank whose details can be changed freely. They are flavor, not canon.

When two real pieces of lore conflict, the goal is a fix that keeps the strongest parts of both, not deleting one. Never quietly turn provisional material into canon.

### Core identity in one paragraph

Verdant Reach is an enormous, ancient land of castles, not countries, inside a larger medieval-fantasy world. Ambient magic there is extremely weak, yet the land is full of magical creatures. Because so little magical energy is available, its mages and knights built a precise, intellectual, low-power tradition: they outthink the world instead of overpowering it. Its castles hold knowledge older than most foreign kingdoms. Its scholar-knights travel everywhere as trusted neutral figures. Its many independent and secret orders make the Reach almost impossible for outsiders to understand or conquer. Outside it lie very different magical civilizations, and beneath everything lies a forgotten arcane-mechanical past whose machines are still running. Through all of this, people still travel, eat honey from local castles, study in libraries, raise dragons and drink tea in inns run by ghosts.

**Tone:** majestic, ancient, melancholic, dark, mysterious, cozy, adventurous, strange, and sometimes funny, but never generic grimdark and never generic whimsy. It is an incredibly old world, and ancient history should feel much larger than the current age. Many ruins are older than today's political systems. Medieval fantasy that can be light or dark. Ancient mystery rather than generic high fantasy. Relic technology that still feels like fantasy, not science fiction. Realistic politics. Many mysteries stay unexplained. The world is monumental and cozy at the same time, and neither side should erase the other.

## The Verdant Reach

### Identity

- **[CANON]** Verdant Reach is the name of both the world project and the land at its heart. It is not Agartha, not "AM" and not a video-game world. See [12-archive.md](12-archive.md).
- **[CANON]** It is a medieval-fantasy setting that can be light or dark. It is built on ancient mystery, has relic machinery beneath or beside the medieval world, keeps realistic politics and old institutions, and lets the fantastic stay mysterious. Strange creatures can be funny or wholesome without turning the setting into parody.
- **[ARCHIVE]** Early working title: *Arcane Mechanica: Riders of the Verdant Reach*. You liked it, but it never became the official title.
- **[CANON]** The Verdant Reach, Still Water and Kolobok can be the same universe (studio lore, [his]). The Still Water note says it plainly: "Same universe as the Verdant Reach."

### Scale and geography

- **[CANON]** The Reach is enormous. It is not just another kingdom.
- **[CANON]** **Castles, not countries.** There are castles in the Verdant Reach but no countries in the continental sense.
- **[CANON]** It contains ancient castles, colossal spires built by distant-past civilizations, huge valleys, alpine mountains, forests, rivers and waterways, ancient ruins, and places where the Reach's normal rules break down.
- **[CANON]** The green-valley alpine castlelands, from the earliest version of the Reach, are one of its famous **heartlands**. This is probably where much of traditional Verdant Knight culture developed. That region has vast green valleys, alpine mountains, castles spread across great distances, beautiful roads and landscapes, libraries, training grounds, travelling educated knights and traditions that are not bloodthirsty. ### Political structure

- **[CANON]** The Reach stays largely outside ordinary continental politics.
- **[CANON]** Its castles and institutions can each be independent centres of power.
- **[CANON]** Some of its customs are thousands of years old. Strict rules and traditions exist that outsiders do not fully understand.
- **[CANON]** **Castle domains.** What early lore called "kingdoms" in the heartland were castle realms, not sovereign nations. A great castle can control villages, farmland, roads, forests, rivers, ports, trade, ancient estates, smaller fortifications, dependent noble houses, local armies, sometimes fleets, and large stretches of territory. To outsiders a castle realm can look like a kingdom, and some are stronger than foreign kingdoms. Verdant people do not see them as nations with borders, nationalism or a centralized state.
- **[CANON]** The Reach is made of overlapping castle domains, ancient noble traditions, knightly orders, local laws, shared customs, old agreements, libraries, independent institutions, secret societies and sacred places.
- **[CANON]** There is no fully defined central Verdant monarchy. How the whole Reach governs itself is still open.
- **[CANON]** When something truly threatens the Reach, separate orders, societies and secret groups can cooperate.
- **[CANON]** There are no major mage cults dominating Verdant society the way magical institutions dominate some foreign kingdoms. Magic is knowledge, not the basis of social rank.

### Why the Reach is almost unbreakable

**[CANON]** You described the Reach as almost unbreakable. The reasons are:

1. Foreign magic becomes drastically weaker there. A spell that would be devastating elsewhere shrinks to a fraction of itself. Powerful foreign mages dislike the Reach because they lose their usual advantage.
2. Its castles are extraordinarily strong.
3. Its institutions are extremely old.
4. Many independent orders began there.
5. Nobody outside knows exactly what all of those orders can do. See [03-knights-and-orders.md](03-knights-and-orders.md).
6. Knowledge has built up over centuries or millennia.
7. When existential danger appears, otherwise separate institutions can unite.
8. The geography itself is difficult.
9. Many ancient creatures and hidden forces live within the Reach.
10. Invaders may not understand the political system well enough to know what they actually need to defeat.

The point is not that the Reach has the biggest army. The point is that an invader cannot know what they are invading. The same structure is why Y cannot dominate the Reach. See [04-the-wider-world.md](04-the-wider-world.md).

- **[CANON]** Features include gardens, roads, trade networks, archives, intricate canals, river access, sophisticated waterways, exceptional architecture, beautiful interiors and amenities, libraries, training areas, artistic craftsmanship, practical engineering and huge defensive capability.
- **[CANON]** Some castles have armies, fleets, very strong conventional defences and rare mythical creatures. They can be almost impossible to take by ordinary military means.

Their forces exist for defence, ancient obligations, monster threats, expeditions, outside invasions, protecting sacred places and fulfilling old treaties, not for expansion. Military power is everywhere in the Reach, but the culture restrains it, which fits knights who are learned and not bloodthirsty.

## Magic

### The defining rule: weak ambient magic, rich magical life

- **[CANON]** Ambient magic is extremely weak in the Verdant Reach. It may be the only major region of its continent with almost no normal ambient magic.
- **[CANON]** This does not mean magic is absent. Special places exist where magic can become extremely powerful, ancient magical structures exist, and mages exist. Everyday magical energy is simply scarce.
- **[CANON]** A powerful continental mage who enters the Reach cannot work at normal strength. A huge spell may shrink to a small fraction of itself. This changes warfare completely.
- **[CANON]** **The Reach is poor in ambient magic but extraordinarily rich in magical life.** There are many mythical and magical creatures there, including dragons, unicorns, ancient beasts, forest creatures, phoenixes, gargoyles and treefolk. This replaces any earlier wording that called magical creatures rare in the Reach.
- **[CANON]** This creates one of the Reach's defining mysteries. A foreign mage expects a land full of magical creatures to overflow with mana, and instead their spells barely work. So where does the creatures' magic come from?
- **[CANON]** Magical creatures do not necessarily depend on the ambient field the way human mages do.
- **[PROVISIONAL]** Possible answers, none chosen yet:
  - Some creatures have internal magical organs or cores.
  - Some descend from an older magical age.
  - Some feed on specific ancient places.
  - Some species use magic in ways humans do not understand.
  - Inherited ancient energy, or specialized biological mechanisms.
  - Feeding on rare magical currents.
  - Symbiosis with magical environments.
- **[CANON]** Don't fully explain this without deliberate worldbuilding. The mystery is useful.

## Creatures and Peoples

**Governing rule [CANON]:** The Verdant Reach is poor in ambient magic but extraordinarily rich in magical life. Many mythical creatures live there. See [02-magic.md](02-magic.md).

Many entries below came from TikTok/visual work and are tagged **[FLAVOR]**. They are usable world texture. Promote any of them to canon whenever you choose.

**Design rule [CANON]:** Strange creatures need ecology and culture, not just combat stats. Ask: What do they eat? Where do they live? Are they intelligent? Do people trade with them, protect them, or live alongside them? How do castles treat them? Do they have customs?

A tag in a heading, or in the line that introduces a list, applies to every untagged bullet under it.

### Great creatures

#### Dragons [CANON]
- Deeply tied to Verdant knightly tradition.
- Most dragons are now gone or much rarer than they once were.
- Young dragons may be confused and need guidance.
- The finest knights can become dragon knights, raising dragons from hatchlings over years. See [03-knights-and-orders.md](03-knights-and-orders.md).
- Great dragons have befriended distinguished knights and allied with kingdoms and castles.
- Dragons live long enough to remember human companions for centuries after they die.
- **Dragons love chess.** Older dragons play on enormous boards carved into the floor, with human-sized pieces. This gives them an intellectual and social side beyond being giant combat animals. It isn't just a joke. It implies intelligence, patience, long memory and a strategic culture.
- Dragons are intelligent beings, not mounts.
- Open: why their numbers declined, where they went, and why freeing the eggs made them return.
- The First Quest ends with dragons sensing freed eggs and starting to return. See [08-characters-and-stories.md](08-characters-and-stories.md).

### The First Quest: The Stolen Dragon Eggs

**[CANON]** This is one of the clearest real stories in the world.

**Backstory.** Centuries earlier, a knight of the order stole dragon eggs. He found that eating them kept him young and delayed ageing. Eventually it drove him mad. The tale survived.

**Sequence.**
1. The tale is told to the red-caped knight when he is young.
2. Years later, he chooses it as his first quest.
3. He searches for the stolen eggs.
4. He discovers ancient ruins.
5. The eggs are hidden inside an enormous egg-shaped stone sculpture.
6. The ancient, corrupted Verdant Knight is still there.
7. They duel.
8. The young knight defeats him.
9. The eggs are freed.
10. Dragons sense the eggs have been released and begin returning.

**Meaning.**
- **[CANON]** The antagonist's unnatural survival from eating dragon eggs is part of what drove him mad.
- **[CANON]** The hero isn't saving the last dragons in existence. The dragons' return gives the quest a larger consequence.
- **[CANON]** It shows that Verdant institutions have their own shameful or dangerous historical figures.

# FILE: 13-writing-guide.md

## Writing Guide

Rules for writing any new Verdant Reach material: lore, stories, characters, factions, locations, dialogue or visual prompts.

### World design principles

1. **Avoid generic fantasy.** No standard evil empire, generic dark lord, generic elf kingdom or generic magic academy. If something familiar exists, give it logic specific to Verdant Reach. Never borrow recognizable signatures from famous works, such as a continent-spanning northern wall (Game of Thrones), a Mordor-like dark land or a Hogwarts-like school. That applies to names, maps and visuals too.
2. **History should feel layered.** Ruins beneath ruins, institutions older than nations, creatures that remember forgotten eras, ancient machines with unknown purposes, conflicting historical accounts.
3. **Power needs context.** Avoid endless escalation where every new threat is just bigger. Different forces are strong in different ways:
   - Verdant mages win through precision.
   - Continental mages win through raw magical output.
   - Northerners survive magical instability with crystals.
   - Ancient machines may resist magic.
   - Some creatures have biological magic.
   - Knightly orders may have strange, specialized abilities.
4. **Mystery is valuable.** Some things stay ancient, ambiguous, misunderstood or only partly documented.
5. **Cultures should feel lived in.** Food, roads, travel, jobs, families, inns, customs, trade, education, humour, rituals. The world isn't only wars and gods.
6. **Strange creatures have ecology and culture**, not just combat roles. See [07-creatures-and-peoples.md](07-creatures-and-peoples.md).
7. **Old but alive.** Verdant Reach is not a dying world. It has functioning societies, trade, families, scholars, young knights, new stories and a future.

### The central tonal contrast

| Monumental | Ordinary |
|---|---|
| Dead gods, ancient civilizations, colossal machines, titans | Travellers, libraries, food, inns |
| Magical catastrophes, lost wars, secret orders | Knight training, families, bakeries, merchants |
| Time anomalies, dragons, forgotten history | Tea, strange little creatures, friendship, local customs |

The ordinary makes the epic feel real, and the epic keeps the ordinary from becoming generic cozy fantasy. Both are essential.

### Instructions for new material

1. Preserve established canon.
2. Flag real contradictions before overwriting old material.
3. Prefer reconciling two good ideas over deleting one.
4. Keep confirmed lore separate from new proposals, using the tags in [README.md](README.md).
5. Don't turn TikTok jokes into foundational world rules without a reason.
6. Don't simplify the magic systems into generic RPG mechanics.
7. Don't automatically explain ancient mysteries.
8. Don't make the protagonist uniquely responsible for everything in history.
9. Verdant Reach is a functioning civilization with many important people besides the protagonist.
10. Add depth sideways (cultures, histories, institutions, ecology, philosophy), not just by making each threat larger.
11. Old lore discussions outrank conveniences from visual prompts.
12. When inventing a major new faction, place, creature or event, check what it implies for existing lore.
13. Prefer connections that make old details more meaningful.
14. Keep Verdant Reach recognizable.
15. Never quietly turn provisional material into canon.
16. Game text is the games' script, not the world's law. Tips, tap lines, item descriptions, captions and endings are tagged **[GAME TEXT]** and stay flavor until the idea under them is confirmed in a lore note. Stats, tiers, available counts, growth rates and skill names in the games are placeholders and never lore. See [17-studio-and-games.md](17-studio-and-games.md).

### The pixel-scenes method

**[CANON]** (the studio's working method, `studio/.claude/skills/pixel-scenes/SKILL.md`.) Every piece is a small software renderer written for that piece: code decides the colour index of every pixel of a 216-pixel-wide canvas (384 to 470 tall by screen shape, or 320 wide for the higher-resolution character sheet and the cards), and the browser scales it up with nearest-neighbour so the pixels stay sharp. The rules are fixed: one hue-shifting 12-colour ramp per scene, dark to light with the hue moving along it (indigo shadow to plum to rose to rust to gold to cream, or navy to teal to mint), plus at most two to five accent colours for things that must pop; clean bands, with 4x4 Bayer dither only across band edges and never raw noise over whole areas; the light source in the frame (a sun, moon, red sun, rift, circle or lantern) with a glow falloff in the sky and rim light on whatever faces it; lots of empty space and one focal point; code draws landscapes (sky, clouds, ridges, water, fields, fog) and hands draw things (characters, buildings and props as text-row sprites, usually silhouettes that get their rim light automatically; round organic things like a fish head or the loaf may be procedural if shaded like 3D forms); motion is small and constant (wind, ripples, blinking eyes, cloth, drifting clouds, birds); text is HTML in Pixelify Sans over the canvas, placed in canvas-pixel units. The workflow is a loop: write a brief (subject, light and its position, the 12 colours, horizon, focal point, what moves, what the player can do), build the static layers once, render frames headless in Node, look at the PNGs like an art director, fix, repeat three to six times, measure speed (under 4 ms a frame on a laptop, with a small deopt count), smoke-test the real page with jsdom, and ship one HTML file while telling you honestly what is weak or untested. Craft rules from the Reachbound README (`studio/pieces/reachbound-muster/README.md`):

- Hand-draw anything that must read as a thing; code-drawn people and buildings look amateur.
- Glints on water are sparse short horizontal dashes; random dots look like a Christmas tree.
- Light pools stay small and dither out; big flat pools look like orange discs.
- Performance: fixed numbers as plain constants, per-frame numbers in typed arrays, no shared field names across hot objects.
