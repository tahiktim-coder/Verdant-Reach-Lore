# Writing Guide

Rules for writing any new Verdant Reach material: lore, stories, characters, factions, locations, dialogue or visual prompts.

## World design principles

1. **Avoid generic fantasy.** No standard evil empire, generic dark lord, generic elf kingdom or generic magic academy. If something familiar exists, give it logic specific to Verdant Reach. Never borrow recognizable signatures from famous works, such as a continent-spanning northern wall (Game of Thrones), a Mordor-like dark land or a Hogwarts-like school. That applies to names, maps and visuals too.
2. **History should feel layered.** Ruins beneath ruins, institutions older than nations, creatures that remember forgotten eras, ancient machines with unknown purposes, conflicting historical accounts.
3. **Power needs context.** Avoid endless escalation where every new threat is just bigger. Different forces are strong in different ways:
   - Verdant mages win through precision.
   - Continental mages win through raw magical output.
   - Northerners survive magical instability with crystals.
   - Ancient machines may resist magic.
   - Some creatures have biological magic.
   - Knightly orders may have strange, specialized abilities.
4. **Mystery is valuable.** Some things stay ancient, ambiguous, misunderstood or only partly documented.
5. **Cultures should feel lived in.** Food, roads, travel, jobs, families, inns, customs, trade, education, humour, rituals. The world isn't only wars and gods.
6. **Strange creatures have ecology and culture**, not just combat roles. See [07-creatures-and-peoples.md](07-creatures-and-peoples.md).
7. **Old but alive.** Verdant Reach is not a dying world. It has functioning societies, trade, families, scholars, young knights, new stories and a future.

## The central tonal contrast

| Monumental | Ordinary |
|---|---|
| Dead gods, ancient civilizations, colossal machines, titans | Travellers, libraries, food, inns |
| Magical catastrophes, lost wars, secret orders | Knight training, families, bakeries, merchants |
| Time anomalies, dragons, forgotten history | Tea, strange little creatures, friendship, local customs |

The ordinary makes the epic feel real, and the epic keeps the ordinary from becoming generic cozy fantasy. Both are essential.

## Instructions for new material

1. Preserve established canon.
2. Flag real contradictions before overwriting old material.
3. Prefer reconciling two good ideas over deleting one.
4. Keep confirmed lore separate from new proposals, using the tags in [README.md](README.md).
5. Don't turn TikTok jokes into foundational world rules without a reason.
6. Don't simplify the magic systems into generic RPG mechanics.
7. Don't automatically explain ancient mysteries.
8. Don't make the protagonist uniquely responsible for everything in history.
9. Verdant Reach is a functioning civilization with many important people besides the protagonist.
10. Add depth sideways (cultures, histories, institutions, ecology, philosophy), not just by making each threat larger.
11. Old lore discussions outrank conveniences from visual prompts.
12. When inventing a major new faction, place, creature or event, check what it implies for existing lore.
13. Prefer connections that make old details more meaningful.
14. Keep Verdant Reach recognizable.
15. Never quietly turn provisional material into canon.
16. Game text is the games' script, not the world's law. Tips, tap lines, item descriptions, captions and endings are tagged **[GAME TEXT]** and stay flavor until the idea under them is confirmed in a lore note. Stats, tiers, available counts, growth rates and skill names in the games are placeholders and never lore. See [17-studio-and-games.md](17-studio-and-games.md).
