# Places

Atlas tiers (Hearthpaths, Wanderer's Roads, Elder Frontiers, Forbidden Roots) are defined in [01-the-reach.md](01-the-reach.md).

## Within the Verdant Reach

### The green-valley heartland
**[CANON]** The famous alpine castlelands: vast green valleys, castles spread across great distances, immense bridges, beautiful roads, libraries and training grounds. This is probably the cradle of Verdant Knight culture. Tier: Hearthpaths and Wanderer's Roads.

### The colossal spires
**[CANON]** Huge spires built by civilizations from the distant past. Their builders and purpose are unknown.

### Places where the rules break
**[CANON]** There are areas where the Reach's normal rules break down. One kind is special places where magic becomes extremely powerful despite the low ambient field.

### The northern mana flowers: Colossal Starblooms
**[CANON]** In the far north of the Reach grow enormous flowers whose cores slowly gather huge amounts of mana. The energy makes them grow to colossal size. Tier: Elder Frontiers.
**[FLAVOR, from refs 30/39]** They are called **Colossal Starblooms**. Their **heart-nectar** (the mana-rich core) heals most diseases or ailments, and a single vial or flask is worth a fortune: "a small castle" or "a king's ransom". This makes them a real economic and political prize.

### The Cavern of Giants
**[CANON]** Holds something like a breach in time. Through a wall or opening, travellers can glimpse the distant past. They see ancient giants on the other side, and the giants appear to be looking back. Whether this is true time travel, memory or something stranger is deliberately unresolved. Tier: Elder Frontiers.

### The Eye-Tree and the Forest of Eyes
**[CANON]** An ancient, cursed tree associated with eyes and watching. Tier: Elder Frontiers.
**[FLAVOR, from ref 03]** **The Forest of Eyes** is a whole forest of trees with watching eyes. It is the only place to harvest **truth-syrup sap**, and the price is that the trees watch every memory you'd rather forget.
**[PROPOSED FIX]** The Eye-Tree is the oldest or central tree of the Forest of Eyes.
**[ARCHIVE]** Assistant embellishment, never confirmed: it was once a guardian oak, and the red embers in its bark "remember exactly how you die."

### The Stone Hands
**[CANON]** Ancient, enormous stone hands survive from the past. Magic stops working within or near their influence. They show that some civilization or force could do things modern societies can't easily reproduce. Tier: Elder Frontiers.

### Legendary warden sites
**[CANON]** Sacred places, ancient halls, dangerous ruins and important seals guarded by legendary knights. Their true significance may not be publicly known.

### The giant toad's swamp
**[CANON]** A giant toad carries knights through swamp country, either with the knights in a boat or riding on or alongside the creature.
**[PROVISIONAL]** Name: Toadswamp. Not firmly established.

### The Endless Sword Fields
**[CANON]** A war cemetery made of weapons instead of gravestones. Fields of swords stretch past the horizon, with a blade for every fallen fighter. The landscape itself records countless wars, and it should feel truly endless.
**[FLAVOR, from ref 21]** One TikTok caption ties it to the Reach ("Verdant Reach grows its cemeteries from steel"), which suggests it could be a Verdant custom rather than one battlefield. It's a loose idea, and the location is open.

### Ghost-run inns
**[FLAVOR]** Inns operated by ghosts who keep serving coffee, tea and travellers. Tier: Hearthpaths.

### The Castle of the Order
**[CANON]** "One castle houses the Verdant Knights order." (studio lore, [his]) See S4 in [10-contradictions-and-fixes.md](10-contradictions-and-fixes.md) for how one castle fits a knighthood the bible spreads across many castles.
**[CANON]** (studio lore) Each castle is unique. **[GAME TEXT]** "Every castle in the Reach is built different." This fits the bible's independent, extraordinary castles.
**[GAME TEXT]** The games name it the "Castle of the Order" (Muster home line for the Verdant Knight; a placeholder name from the studio). The Hero of the Reach sheet calls Sir Aldren "Knight of the Verdant Order" and puts him in a candlelit stone room with an arched window that looks out on the Reach at dusk: rolling hills, stars, a far castle on the horizon. "Every castle paints its own. This is the Order's leaf."
**[FLAVOR]** The Muster shows it as a far castle on its own hill, hazy at dawn, a tall central tower flanked by two smaller towers on a wide base, seen across a valley from the knight's hill. Card 13 shows a knight on a crenellated stone wall at night, counting his years, with something tall waiting on the far ridge.
Location within the Reach: open. Tier: Hearthpaths for the castle itself; the covenant makes it a strange one.

### The roads and the wheat country
**[CANON]** (studio lore) The invisible loaf terrorises roads and fields. See [07-creatures-and-peoples.md](07-creatures-and-peoples.md) and [16-kolobok.md](16-kolobok.md).
**[FLAVOR, from Kolobok, Reach Field Tips and the Muster]** The region the games keep returning to: wheat in strip fields, waist-high at harvest; a dirt road with cart ruts running from the viewer's hill into the far distance; a windmill on the near ridge; a village with its own flour stores; a lake at the horizon; standing stones on hilltops and at field edges; meadows and post-and-rail fences with crows; far hazy hills; hilltop castles with lit windows; a castle wall under the moon; a low sun. In Kolobok the viewer stands on a hill at dusk, where a stone elder leans with four pebble folk beside it. You loved the Kolobok valley at sunset (studio art direction).
**[GAME TEXT]** (Hero of the Reach) Flour sacks are "for the roads at harvest", so the loaf is worst, or most watched for, at harvest.
**[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Road wardens hang "flour bells", sacks of flour on poles over the road, so the loaf bursts them and shows itself. A burst one is a snapped pole and an empty, torn sack. Who the road wardens are is undefined.
**[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Masons were paid per stone to break field stones blamed for the loaf's damage.
**[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Stone folk are "field and boundary stones" of this country; pebble folk live on the Still Water shore (studio Kolobok note). See [07-creatures-and-peoples.md](07-creatures-and-peoples.md).
**[PROPOSED FIX]** This is a lowland of the Reach, distinct from the alpine heartland. See S1.
Tier: Hearthpaths most days; Wanderer's Roads when the loaf is about.

### Library doors and the Collector's ruins
**[CANON]** The Library of the Librarians vanished suddenly and magically, still in the Old Age (studio lore, [his]). Reincarnated librarians could no longer reach it or remember it. Collectors follow visions toward it and uncover other mysteries instead. See [04-the-wider-world.md](04-the-wider-world.md) and [07-creatures-and-peoples.md](07-creatures-and-peoples.md).
**[GAME TEXT]** "If you find a door standing alone in a field, look through it. Don't walk through it. The Library isn't there anymore." (Reach Field Tips, card 4)
**[GAME TEXT]** (Muster) The Collector's home is "The ruins". His line: "Not this door either."
**[FLAVOR]** Card 4 shows a stone arch of worn blocks alone in a night meadow. Through it: a golden afternoon sky, a small cloud lit by a sun you can't see, and a strip of wheat from somewhere else. Motes drift out of the doorway, and the hooded lantern figure of the Collector stands beside it. The Muster shows the Collector by a ruined arch of two worn pillars and a keystone on a hill at night, with "stars that shouldn't be there" inside it.
**[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** What the studio pieces imply, never stated outright: lone arches in fields are old doors to the Library, still opening onto somewhere, but never onto the Library. Your note says only that the Library vanished; the doors are the games' framing. Whether they stand only in the Reach, or anywhere in the world, is open.
Tier: Elder Frontiers.

### False castles
**[GAME TEXT]** "Every castle in the Reach is built different. If two look the same, one of them isn't a castle." (card 8)
**[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Card 8 shows two identical hilltop castles. The card's code marks the right one as "not a castle: its gate breathes, a window looks back", one window turning into a mint-green eye. What a false castle is (a creature, a glamour, a trap) is undefined. See [07-creatures-and-peoples.md](07-creatures-and-peoples.md).
Tier: Wanderer's Roads at least.

### The necromancer's circle
**[CANON]** Verdant Reach knights from some castle fought a necromancer who summoned a projection of the cursed warrior. "Where the fight happened (outside the Reach, or a cave inside it) is deliberately left unknown." (studio lore, [his]) Keep it unknown; it is listed under the Reach for convenience only.
**[GAME TEXT]** The Muster gives the Projection the home "A necromancer's circle" (its growth cell reads 'one per ritual', a placeholder stat) and shows a cave whose mouth frames the scene, no sun, a glowing green circle on the cave floor with six points orbiting it, sparks rising, and the necromancer small and hunched at the left, arms raised. See the Projection in [07-creatures-and-peoples.md](07-creatures-and-peoples.md).
**[PROVISIONAL]** The projection holds about 1/1000 of the warrior's power (studio lore, [working]). See [08-characters-and-stories.md](08-characters-and-stories.md).

## Elsewhere in the world

### Hourglass Isle
**[CANON]** An island tied to time. Possible phenomena include rewinding part of a person's life, showing possible futures, and revealing futures someone fears. The exact mechanics are open.
**[FLAVOR, from ref 10]** One TikTok image shows it **floating inside frozen time**, hanging in the sky above land. This is a loose idea, so it could float or sit at sea. It can rewind a single life, or show every future you were too afraid to take. You've used the name yourself, so it's no longer only an assistant suggestion.
**[FLAVOR, from ref 39]** Possibly related: a field of giant standing hourglasses on open ground.

### Northern sacred-tree groves and storm valleys
**[CANON]** See [04-the-wider-world.md](04-the-wider-world.md).

### The Silent Battlefield (great abandoned warland)
**[CANON]** concept / **[PROVISIONAL]** name. See [04-the-wider-world.md](04-the-wider-world.md).

### Clockwork towers
**[CANON]** Towers left by an earlier civilization, not built in the modern era. They may hold ancient machinery, relics, hidden mechanisms and forgotten history. Their deeper purpose is unknown.
**[FLAVOR, from refs 35/38]** They are "scars from the old age". Their **catacombs** always hide something epic, and never give it up for free. The first sign you're near one is gears jutting out of the ground. Keep walking and the earth itself starts to tick, which hints that their machinery runs underground far beyond the tower.

### Teleport lighthouses
**[FLAVOR, from ref 15]** Old lighthouses whose doors teleport you. They are safest when the **lighthouse crabs** are away. The door works, but you might not like where it leads. (This replaces the archived assistant idea "giant lighthouse".)

### The machine under the southern mountain
**[PROVISIONAL]** See [05-ancient-history.md](05-ancient-history.md).

### Mountain lairs of the fire dragons
**[CANON]** Very powerful fire dragons live in mountains. See [07-creatures-and-peoples.md](07-creatures-and-peoples.md).

### Storm-wyrm nests
**[CANON]** Certain storm systems contain nests of enormous wyrms. See [07-creatures-and-peoples.md](07-creatures-and-peoples.md).

### Cloud kingdoms
**[PROVISIONAL]** Cloud realms that angel-like beings come down from. Only fragments of this idea exist.

### Still Water
**[CANON]** The lake of the fishing-horror game. "Same universe as the Verdant Reach." (studio lore, [his]) You catch a talking fish that grants wishes; then the sun sets and a menacing red sun rises (studio lore, [his]). The fish and the red sun are in [07-creatures-and-peoples.md](07-creatures-and-peoples.md); the story is in [08-characters-and-stories.md](08-characters-and-stories.md); the full game is in [15-still-water.md](15-still-water.md).
**[GAME TEXT]** The games use Still Water as the lake's own name: the Muster homes "Still Water" and "Still Water shore", and card 3, "Still Water under the red sun" (the lake mirroring a red sky, a boat, a fisher, a line, rings on the water). Opening line: "The lake is so still it could be glass." The fish grants wishes "word for word", and the red sun "is not a sun" (a Still Water caption, also in the story map). Setting shown: a glass-still lake among mountains, fished from a rowing boat with a lantern; a tall peak on the right, a ridge on the left, far ridges at the horizon, one boat. Birds cross by day and fish jump. A cabin with a lit window appears on the far shore if you wish for a home: "Done. Look, a light on the shore." "Back so soon? The lake remembers every wish." Fish caught there: glass perch, mirror char, blue smelt, fjord trout, needle eel, pale grayling. "It smells of snow." (grayling card) So it reads as a cold mountain lake.
**[GAME TEXT]** The "Still water" ending: "You row until the water is only water. You never fish here again. Some evenings, the sunset looks back." The prototype has three wishes and three endings (Home, Dark, Still water).
**[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** (Still Water story map, design draft 7, a studio design document, not the built prototype) "Nothing on the lake is moving except you." "Every light out here is for someone." "The lake keeps count." "Nobody comes here twice." It adds an ocean branch: a wish takes the boat onto open sea where the mountains sink into haze, huge shadows pass, and one settles under the boat, "waiting longer than you have".
**[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** Pebble folk live on its shore, and the old woman who baked Kolobok got her wish from its fish (studio notes, proposed by the studio's Claude). The Muster shows eight pebble folk on a stony shore under the moon; Field Tip 6 shows a lakeshore of blinking pebbles at night. If this holds, Still Water is within walking distance of the Reach's wheat country and the loaf's roads. The Kolobok valley shows an unnamed lake at the horizon; whether that lake is Still Water is open.
Location: open. It is listed under "Elsewhere in the world" only because nothing places it inside the Reach yet; move it if you decide otherwise. Tier: Elder Frontiers, or Forbidden Roots once the red sun rises.

### The eastern kingdom's city
**[GAME TEXT]** (Field Tip 9) "If a kingdom throws you a feast the night you arrive, count the exits before the second course." The card is titled "the eastern kingdom's welcome feast".
**[FLAVOR, from card 9]** A city of towers and domes along the horizon at night, every window lit, a lamp-lined road leading to it, fireworks, "a welcome too warm". Whether it is the capital is not said. The kingdom itself is your lore for [04-the-wider-world.md](04-the-wider-world.md). See S3 on where it sits.

### The three guild capitals
**[CANON]** The Librarians' order became three guilds, Mages, Engineers and Collectors, "each with its own capital today" (studio lore, [his]). None is named or placed. See [04-the-wider-world.md](04-the-wider-world.md).

### The pass of the last stand
**[CANON]** From the Old Age legend of the cursed sword: the warrior's realm "was attacked from all sides; one mountain pass led to the capital; odds of 1000 to 1." He held the pass alone for months "until the corpse piles and stench made the enemy withdraw." (studio lore, [his]) The realm, the pass and the capital are unnamed and unplaced; the era is the Old Age. See [05-ancient-history.md](05-ancient-history.md) and [08-characters-and-stories.md](08-characters-and-stories.md).

## Outside the world

### The mountain of Old magic (the Summit)
**[CANON]** The rally scroll "sends you to a parallel dimension of Old magic: a near-infinite, symmetrical, gradually sloped walkable mountain. You arrive above stormy magical clouds with the colossal summit (site of an ancient godly battle) visible." (studio lore, [his])
**[PROVISIONAL]** "The summit is visible thousands of km away, marked by green lightning or similar." (studio lore, [working])
**[CANON]** The cursed warrior is still there, walking toward the summit "for centuries or millennia" (studio lore, [his]). See [08-characters-and-stories.md](08-characters-and-stories.md). The legend itself belongs in [05-ancient-history.md](05-ancient-history.md).
**[GAME TEXT]** The Summit Road calls it "the endless mountain of Old magic" and bills the legend as "A legend from before the Old Age". Two of its captions: "So he walks. Green lightning marks the summit." "He has been walking for a thousand years." (all six are in [17-studio-and-games.md](17-studio-and-games.md))
Note: the piece calls him only "a warrior" and "he", never a knight, and never says "cursed sword" or "parallel dimension"; those words are your note's. "A thousand years" is the piece's wording; your note says centuries or millennia. See S9.
**[GAME TEXT]** "If you see green lightning over a mountain no map shows, don't read any scroll near it." (card 10) The card shows a single peak at night with a green glow and green bolts at its summit. This implies the summit, or something like it, can be seen from the ordinary world; that is not in your notes. See S10: a mountain in another dimension should not be visible from a Reach field, so either the tip is folklore or the dimension shows through somewhere.
**[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** The Summit Road shows a paved slab road with worn joints along the crest of a symmetric ridge of ancient terraces, running toward a colossal hazy spire with faint ancient bands crowned by green-white lightning; colossal swords planted point-down flanking the road, many times a walker's height ("relics: colossal blades left from the war of gods", code comment); a cloud sea on the flanks; storm banks mirrored about the summit; green motes of Old magic rising; the mountain hums. The walker is seen from behind, caped, dragging a glowing sword. You liked this piece's lighting, not the rest, so treat these details as unconfirmed.
**[PROPOSED FIX]** Keep this dimension separate from the Parallel-Dimension Order's near-frozen one in [03-knights-and-orders.md](03-knights-and-orders.md) unless you link them.
Note: this is the third dimension beside the world in the lore, with the Parallel-Dimension Order's near-frozen dimension (bible) and the place the Covenant's Hunter blinks into for a few seconds while twenty years pass (studio). All three bend time. The sky jellyfish's parallel realm (ref 05) is a further candidate. Whether they are one place is open; see [11-open-questions.md](11-open-questions.md).
Tier: Forbidden Roots.

## Homes named in the Muster (game text)

**[GAME TEXT]** The Muster roster gives each creature a "home" line. These are placeholders by the studio's Claude: Pebble Folk, "Still Water shore"; Loaf Crows, "Wherever it rolls"; Stone Elder, "Field stones"; Verdant Knight, "Castle of the Order"; The Collector, "The ruins"; The Fish, "Still Water"; The Projection, "A necromancer's circle"; The Covenant's Hunter, "Between moments"; Kolobok, "The roads". Use them as the games' script, not as settled geography.
