# Verdant Knights and the Orders

## Verdant Knights

- **[CANON]** After low magic, the knights are the second main pillar of the setting.
- **[CANON]** Verdant Knights are more than warriors. They are generally highly educated, skilled fighters, scholars and travellers, and they guard accumulated knowledge. They are tied to ancient castles and orders and are expected to understand history, culture and magic.
- **[CANON]** A Verdant Knight is expected to be some mix of warrior, scholar, traveller, historian, investigator, guardian, diplomat, explorer and keeper of old knowledge. Education matters a great deal in their culture. A knight should know much more than swordsmanship.
- **[CANON]** They travel between castles, libraries, archives, training grounds, foreign courts, dangerous ruins, remote communities and ancient sacred places.
- **[CANON]** Their tradition is knowledgeable and not bloodthirsty.
- **[CANON]** There are many Verdant Knights. They belong to a functioning civilization, not a dying remnant. The red-caped protagonist is not the last of them. See [08-characters-and-stories.md](08-characters-and-stories.md).
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) One castle houses **the Verdant Knights order**. The studio roster calls its home the **Castle of the Order** and the Hero sheet calls it the **Verdant Order**; both names are the studio's, not yours. This sits uneasily beside the many knights at many castles above. See S4 in [10-contradictions-and-fixes.md](10-contradictions-and-fixes.md). Both versions stay until you decide.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) Each castle in the Reach is unique. **[GAME TEXT]** (from Reach Field Tips, card 8) “Every castle in the Reach is built different. If two look the same, one of them isn’t a castle.” **[GAME TEXT]** (from Hero of the Reach) “Every castle paints its own. This is the Order’s leaf.” So in the game each castle has its own painted shield device, and the covenant order’s device is a leaf.
- **[GAME TEXT]** (from Reach Field Tips, card 13) “Knights of the Order don’t celebrate birthdays. Ask one why, and he’ll tell you how many years he has left.” Usable as a folk saying. It brushes against the rule that nobody can predict the Hunter. See S12.

### Numbers and structure

- **[CANON]** Major castles might have around 3 to 7 important knights, sometimes as many as 20.
- **[FLAVOR]** Travelling or operational groups of at least four heavily armoured knights are common. Compatible with canon; see P1 in [10-contradictions-and-fixes.md](10-contradictions-and-fixes.md).
- **[PROPOSED FIX]** These two numbers don't conflict. The 3 to 20 figure counts a castle's elite resident knights. The groups of four or more are travelling patrols and expeditions, which can include lesser or junior knights.

### Abroad

- **[CANON]** Verdant Knights are found in almost every country, even though the Reach itself stays out of foreign politics.
- **[CANON]** Abroad they act as envoys, advisors, guardians, mediators and trusted neutral figures. Expanded roles: scholar, investigator, monster hunter, guest of a foreign court, keeper of an ancient agreement, protector of an archive, teacher, observer, or representative of a particular castle or order.
- **[CANON]** They are trusted because they normally aren't there to annex anything.

### Career

- **[CANON]** **Quest experience.** A knight who survives about ten years of quests may finally come across genuinely legendary relics. Time served doesn't earn the relic. Surviving long enough brings them into contact with extraordinary things. (The idea began as game-style "epic loot" talk, but the world idea underneath it is real.)
- **[PROVISIONAL]** Example relic: a storm-forged lightning sword.
- **[CANON]** **Legendary knights become wardens.** Knights who become truly legendary may stop wandering and be assigned to guard essential places: sacred sites, ancient halls, ancient vaults, dangerous ruins, important seals, magical anomalies, forgotten machines and historical sites, whose true significance may not be public. A truly legendary knight can become a permanent warden.
- **[FLAVOR, from ref 08]** **Part-time scarecrow knights:** knights in full armour and straw hats, paid to scare crows from the fields, who usually end up gossiping with them instead. Not every knight's job is grand.
- **[FLAVOR, from ref 24]** **Frost-speaking knights:** some legendary Verdant Knights "speak frost", and they're the ones you bring to banish a mountain fire dragon. This turns the provisional frost-knight scene into a kind of legendary knight.
- **[FLAVOR]** **Quest-delivery knights.** Not every heavily armoured knight fights on the front line. Some, in exceptional armour on fast horses, carry quest messages. This implies a fairly sophisticated communication network among the orders. **[FLAVOR, from ref 16]** "Some knights never see battle, but their letters on a fast horse still move armies." The image shows the blonde knight with a white horned horse, possibly a noble's unicorn.
- **[FLAVOR, from ref 09]** **The academy:** noble children attend an academy, and castle eagles carry them there. **[PROPOSED FIX]** Per the writing guide's "no generic magic academy" rule, this should be a specifically Verdant institution, probably where future knights are first educated. Nothing else about it is defined.
- Open: what makes someone a Verdant Knight, including recruitment and initiation. See [11-open-questions.md](11-open-questions.md).

## Unicorns and Verdant nobility

- **[CANON]** Unicorns exist in the Verdant Reach, but **magical unicorns traditionally belong to the nobility of the Verdant Knights.**
- **[CANON]** This is a cultural and social tradition. Unicorns may be tied to noble lineages, knightly houses, ancient oaths, specific castles, high status and old traditions.
- **[CANON]** Ordinary people rarely see them, not because only a few exist but because access is tied to Verdant nobility, ancient knightly houses, status, tradition and probably inheritance.
- **[CANON]** Unicorns are never cheap decoration or common fantasy livestock. They are prestigious and meaningful, and they may be intelligent and judgemental.
- **[FLAVOR]** "Unicorns don't guard gates here. They judge you." This is TikTok wording for a real tradition, not the definition of the lore.
- **[NOT CANON]** Common decorative unicorns.
- **[NOT CANON]** "Unicorns are extremely rare creatures that occasionally belong to castles." This was replaced by the nobility rule above.

## Dragon knights

- **[CANON]** Dragons are deeply tied to Verdant knightly tradition.
- **[CANON]** Some of the finest Verdant Knights become dragon knights. They raise and train dragons from youth or from the hatchling stage, which takes years.
- **[CANON]** Dragon knights train alongside their dragons and form lifelong bonds. Experienced dragon-riding knights can cover enormous distances.
- **[CANON]** Dragons are intelligent beings, not mounts.
- **[CANON]** Historically, great dragons sometimes befriended distinguished knights or allied with kingdoms and castles. Dragons can remember human companions for centuries after those humans die.

See also: full dragon lore in [07-creatures-and-peoples.md](07-creatures-and-peoples.md).

## The Covenant and the Covenant's Hunter

- **[CANON]** (studio lore) On one of their epic quests the Verdant Knights order escaped an inescapable death. Since then they are bound by a **covenant**. The studio roster puts it as **[GAME TEXT]** “Escaped a death that cannot be escaped. The covenant keeps it waiting.”
- **[PROVISIONAL]** (studio lore, working) Origin: the knights used **wordplay** when entering the covenant, during a quest they were nearly losing, and it worked. That is why things work the way they do now. You keep this as a concept to refine later.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) A creature manifests unpredictably to kill a targeted knight with its **death weapon**. No one can predict when.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) From the creature's side it is **one continuous fight**: it fights, blinks into a parallel dimension for a few seconds, then resumes, while for the knight **20 years or more** may have passed. No one knows how it works.
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) If anything else tries to kill the knight, the creature stops it. It seems vital to the creature that it lands the finishing blow. Its motives are otherwise unknown.
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** The studio's Claude named it **the Covenant's Hunter**, home “Between moments”. Not your name. **[GAME TEXT]** Muster lore line: “One continuous fight on its side. Twenty years between blows on yours.” Tap lines: “Twenty years pass.” and “It lands the last blow. Nothing else may.” Knight tap lines: “The covenant holds.” and “Not today.” The shipped line and studio/lore/muster-roster.md both add “one knight at a time”; the nine-creature piece's lore/roster.md drops it. It is a studio phrase, [GAME TEXT], not yours.
- **[GAME TEXT]** (from Hero of the Reach) The covenant has a status, shown as “Covenant: bound”, and a record of when the Hunter last came: “The Hunter last came 20 years ago.” Sir Aldren says “The Hunter came twenty years ago. It will come again. Until then, the road.”
- **[GAME TEXT]** (from Reach Field Tips, card 7) “If a knight goes grey in the middle of a sentence, step away. Something just blinked in.” The card shows a tall hooded thing with two green points for eyes fading in behind the traveller with a green bolt beside it, and the traveller's hair turns grey. Card 13 shows the same hooded figure, smaller, waiting on a far ridge while a knight stands on a wall.
- **[GAME TEXT]** The Muster portrait shows the Hunter as a hooded, broad-shouldered silhouette with two pale points in the hood, a long haft ending in a curve of pale light (“the death weapon” in the code), stepping through a thin vertical rift on a plain. A tree beside it grows one stage each time it blinks, with the caption “Twenty years pass.” Studio art, not settled lore.
- **[PROPOSED FIX]** The Hunter's “parallel dimension for a few seconds” and the parallel-dimension order's near-frozen dimension could be the same place, or deliberately different. See S11. Do not merge without a decision.
- **[PROPOSED FIX]** Relation to legendary wardens: a knight who has survived the Hunter once would be a natural candidate for a warden post. Nothing in the studio says this. Suggestion only.

## The Parallel-Dimension Order

- **[CANON]** An ancient order of magic knights began in the Verdant Reach.
- **[CANON]** At some point the order moved itself into a parallel dimension where time is almost frozen.
- **[CANON]** Before disappearing, they made physical vessels and spread them around the world: heavy armour, shields, swords and different specialized suits.
- **[CANON]** These objects let members of the order return to the normal world when needed. Armour that looks empty or ceremonial in some old hall may actually be a doorway for one of these knights.
- **[CANON]** The order stays closely connected with the knights of the Verdant Reach. Its existence is one reason attacking the Reach is so unpredictable.
- **[CANON]** Its name, the dimension's name, the armour categories, its limits and why it left are all unconfirmed. Don't invent definite answers without deliberate development.
- **[ARCHIVE]** Assistant-proposed details you never adopted:
  - Names for the order: the Order Between, the Intercalary, the Fold-Riders, Knights Palimpsest.
  - Name for the dimension: the Interstice.
  - Suit classes: War-Shells, Work-Shells, Civic-Shells, Beast-Shells, Ritual-Shells.
  - Limits: Memory Tithe, Time Drift, Name Silence, dependence on the suits.
- Open: why they left, and how activating a suit works. See [11-open-questions.md](11-open-questions.md).
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) Two other parallel dimensions now exist in the world: the **parallel dimension of Old magic** the rally scroll sends you to (an endless walkable mountain), and the place the Covenant's Hunter blinks into between blows. Neither is stated to be this order's near-frozen dimension. See S11 and [08-characters-and-stories.md](08-characters-and-stories.md).
- **[PROPOSED FIX]** Keep three distinct places unless you choose otherwise: the order's frozen dimension (time almost stops), the Hunter's gap (seconds there, twenty years here), and the mountain of Old magic (time runs normally, the walker ages but cannot die). The order's and the Hunter's dimensions share the same rate (seconds inside, decades outside), which is why S11 asks whether they are one place; the mountain of Old magic is the odd one out because time runs normally there. P10 already links the sky jellyfish's realm to the order's dimension; that stays a separate question.

## Other orders and groups

- **[CANON]** Many independent orders and secret societies exist in the Reach, and outsiders don't know how many there are or what they can do. Almost none have names yet.
- **[CANON]** Orders may be tied to particular castles or independent, extremely old, secretive, specialized, international, or devoted to specific duties.
- **[PROVISIONAL]** **Card-themed knights.** An order connected to playing-card symbolism, with heart, spade and other suit motifs on their armour. This is your concept. **[FLAVOR, from ref 01]** Name: **Destiny Knights**, silent guards who only move "when fate plays a new card". They're shown off duty in a cozy kitchen, laughing, with hearts on their armour. This is the first name for the order that came from you, not from me. Assistant name ideas, none adopted: Order of the Suits, Deckborne Knights, House of Red & Black, Cardinal Smiths, Croupiers of Oath.
- **[FLAVOR, from ref 33]** **Road-mages:** tall wanderers in white hats. If one buys your snacks and shakes your hand, you're safe from bandits until sunrise. **[PROPOSED FIX]** They hold some agreement with the Honest Bandit Guild, and the handshake is a sign the guild respects.
- **[PROVISIONAL]** **The Honest Bandit Guild.** Bandits organized as a real guild with a strict code of honesty. The interest is in the contradiction: they really are bandits, yet unusually trustworthy within the rules of their own trade. Details are undefined.
- **[PROVISIONAL]** **Treefolk who grow knights.** Ancient treefolk cultivate knights over centuries. It is unexplained but highly distinctive, so keep it. See [07-creatures-and-peoples.md](07-creatures-and-peoples.md).
- **[PROVISIONAL]** **Cloud-realm beings** come down looking specifically for Verdant Knights as companions or allies. **[FLAVOR, from ref 13]** These are the **cloudborn heralds**. When one descends from the sky kingdoms, something is about to end or begin, and the first thing she does is look for a Verdant Knight. See [07-creatures-and-peoples.md](07-creatures-and-peoples.md).
- **[CANON]** (studio lore) **The Librarians' order and its three guilds** (Mages, Engineers, Collectors), founded by reincarnating librarians after the Library vanished. Full entry in [05-ancient-history.md](05-ancient-history.md), under Old Age: the Librarians and the Library that vanished.
- **[STUDIO PROPOSAL]** **Road wardens** hang flour bells (sacks on poles) over roads against the invisible loaf. A studio Claude idea, not yours. See [16-kolobok.md](16-kolobok.md).
